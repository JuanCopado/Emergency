# Special-population modules

Shared gate: adjust thresholds, communication, consent, safeguarding, doses and
disposition to physiology, developmental stage, pregnancy and baseline function.

## pediatric-emergencies

- Use pediatric assessment triangle/ABCDE, age-specific vitals and weight in kilograms; use measured weight or a validated estimate when necessary.
- Calculate every high-risk drug and fluid dose with units, maximum cap, concentration and independent check. Use current pediatric resuscitation guidance.
- Consider safeguarding and family communication. Lower the threshold for pediatric critical-care input when trajectory, age or resources increase risk.





## pediatric-procedural-sedation

- Scope: procedural sedation/analgesia in children in ED/acute care. This is **not** RSI and not continuous PICU sedation. Define the procedure goal: anxiolysis, analgesia, immobility, amnesia or dissociation; use non-pharmacologic support and local/regional anaesthesia whenever appropriate.
- **Safety prerequisites:** trained pediatric sedation clinician, airway-skilled staff, resuscitation equipment immediately available, baseline HR/RR/BP/SpO2, continuous observation of airway/chest movement, pulse oximetry and cardiorespiratory monitoring appropriate to depth/agent. Combined opioids + sedatives increase respiratory risk.
- **Ketamine IV:** RCH procedural sedation guidance: initial **1--1.5 mg/kg IV over 1--2 min** immediately before the procedure; additional **0.25--0.5 mg/kg IV every 10 min** if required. Maximum cumulative **4.5 mg/kg**, but doses >2.5 mg/kg are associated with more adverse events and should trigger reconsideration of the strategy.
- **Ketamine IM:** initial **4 mg/kg IM**; a repeat **2 mg/kg IM after 10 min** may be given if sedation is inadequate. RCH lists a maximum cumulative reference of **6 mg/kg**. Ketamine procedural sedation is absolutely contraindicated in infants **<3 months** in this guideline and requires senior review in infants <12 months and in higher-risk airway/respiratory/cardiac situations.
- **Ketamine monitoring/recovery:** expect dissociation, nystagmus and mild HR/BP rise. Be prepared for vomiting, transient laryngospasm and rare apnea/respiratory depression. Do not discharge until the child has returned to premorbid neurologic baseline and can mobilize/verbalize at baseline level as developmentally appropriate.
- **Nitrous oxide/oxygen:** useful for short painful/distressing procedures; RCH supports continuous-flow mixtures titratable **30--70% nitrous oxide**, and states it is safe to start at **70%** when appropriately monitored. A 50:50 premixed nitrous/oxygen system may be used on demand. Procedures >30 min have more vomiting/side effects.
- **Intranasal fentanyl:** for children **>=12 months** with moderate/severe pain and no IV access, RCH: first dose **1.5 micrograms/kg IN**; repeat **0.75--1.5 micrograms/kg after 5--10 min** if needed. Product/concentration matters: RCH currently describes **300 micrograms/mL** hospital nasal solution for lower-volume delivery; do not automatically use an older 50 micrograms/mL IV presentation intranasally without verifying the product.
- **Opioid rescue:** excessive sedation/respiratory depression requires airway support and naloxone according to the pediatric opioid-toxicity pathway. Do not rely on naloxone in place of ventilation.
- **Agent selection:** ketamine is appropriate for short painful procedures needing immobilisation (for example fracture reduction, laceration repair, abscess drainage, foreign-body removal). Nitrous oxide is suited to short procedures with modest analgesic needs. Midazolam provides anxiolysis/amnesia but limited analgesia; do not substitute it for analgesia in a painful procedure.
- **Fasting:** urgency and aspiration risk should be considered, but time-critical procedures must not be delayed solely to meet an arbitrary fasting interval. Follow current pediatric sedation/anaesthesia guidance and document risk-benefit.
- **Failure/escalation:** if target sedation cannot be achieved safely within the source dose envelope, stop stacking doses and reassess the plan; involve anaesthesia for deeper sedation/general anaesthesia when required.
## pediatric-acute-asthma

- Scope: acute asthma/wheeze in children where asthma is the working diagnosis. Distinguish bronchiolitis, foreign body, anaphylaxis, pneumonia and upper-airway obstruction. Treat immediately while assessing severity.
- **Life-threatening features:** drowsiness/confusion/exhaustion, cyanosis, silent or poorly ventilated chest, poor respiratory effort, bradycardia/hypotension, refractory hypoxaemia or impending arrest -> call PICU/anaesthesia early and prepare for invasive airway management.
- **Oxygen:** use controlled oxygen when hypoxaemic. GINA 2026 uses a pediatric target of at least about **92%** in children <=5 years in the initial management box; older-child acute-asthma algorithms target approximately **94% or higher**. Avoid delaying bronchodilator therapy while refining the exact target.
- **Salbutamol/albuterol:** pMDI + spacer is preferred when feasible. For children <=5 years, GINA 2026: **4 or more puffs of 100 micrograms/puff**, one puff at a time, or **2.5 mg nebulized**; in moderate/severe exacerbation repeat about every **20 min for up to 3 doses**, then reassess. For children 6--11 years, GINA acute-exacerbation guidance uses **4--10 puffs** by pMDI + spacer every 20 min for up to 3 doses in the first hour.
- **Ipratropium:** for moderate/severe exacerbation or poor initial response, GINA 2026 supports **4 puffs of 20 micrograms** by pMDI/spacer or **250 micrograms nebulized** with SABA, up to 3 doses about 20--30 min apart in the first hour.
- **Systemic corticosteroid <=5 years:** for moderately severe/severe exacerbation, prednisolone **1--2 mg/kg/day**; maximum **20 mg/day if <2 years** and **30 mg/day if age 2--5 years**, usually **3--5 days**. Alternative dexamethasone **0.3--0.6 mg/kg, max 12 mg**, 1 dose with or without a second dose the next day.
- **Systemic corticosteroid 6--11 years:** prednisone/prednisolone **1--2 mg/kg/day, max 40 mg/day**, usually **3--5 days**; oral dexamethasone **0.3--0.6 mg/kg, max 12 mg**, total 1--2 days.
- **IV magnesium sulfate:** for children **>=2 years** with severe acute asthma and inadequate response after initial therapy, GINA 2026 supports **40--50 mg/kg IV, max 2 g**, by slow infusion over **20--60 min**. It is second-line, not routine for mild/moderate attacks.
- Monitor response after each bronchodilator cycle: work of breathing, speech, air entry, SpO2, HR, mental state and fatigue. Check potassium, glucose and lactate when beta-agonist exposure is high or deterioration is unexplained.
- Avoid sedatives in a spontaneously breathing child with severe asthma. Routine antibiotics, mucolytics and IV aminophylline are not standard first-line therapy. IM adrenaline is for anaphylaxis/angioedema, not routine isolated asthma.
- Escalate for failure to improve within the first hour, oxygen saturation <92%, increasing fatigue, altered consciousness, silent chest, refractory hypoxaemia/hypercapnia or need for SABA more often than every 1--2 h. NIV must not delay intubation.
- **Discharge:** sustained clinical improvement, stable oxygenation on room air, bronchodilator spacing appropriate for home, caregiver competence, inhaler/spacer technique, written action plan and follow-up. Ensure an **ICS-containing controller strategy** appropriate to age/severity; GINA 2026 continues to emphasize anti-inflammatory treatment rather than SABA-only long-term management.

## pediatric-acute-respiratory-support

- Scope: acute oxygenation/ventilation support in infants and children beyond neonatal transition. Combine with the cause-specific pathway (bronchiolitis, asthma, pneumonia, sepsis, neuromuscular disease, cardiac disease, intoxication, etc.). No single respiratory rate, SpO2 or blood-gas value substitutes for trajectory and work of breathing.
- **Initial oxygen:** ERC/RCUK 2025: in respiratory, circulatory or neurological failure, start **100% oxygen** initially, then titrate FiO2 as soon as reliable SpO2 is available. In previously healthy children target **SpO2 94--98%**, using the lowest FiO2 that achieves at least about 94%. Individualise targets in cyanotic congenital heart disease or chronic respiratory failure.
- **Conventional oxygen failure:** consider humidified high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO/HFNC) or CPAP/NIV when hypoxaemia does not respond adequately to conventional oxygen and the child retains sufficient respiratory drive. Do not allow non-invasive support to delay intubation in exhaustion, deteriorating consciousness, refractory hypoxaemia, shock or inability to protect the airway.
- **HFNO flow by weight (RCH):** for **<=12 kg use 2 L/kg/min**. For **>12 kg**, use **2 L/kg/min for the first 12 kg + 0.5 L/kg/min for each kg above 12 kg**, maximum **50 L/min**. Increase to prescribed flow over a few minutes/as tolerated.
- **HFNO oxygen:** RCH starts high-flow system flow in room air (21%) and then adds oxygen according to saturation/clinical need. Do not assume high flow automatically means high FiO2.
- **HFNO response gate:** if there is no clinical stabilisation within **2 h**, obtain PICU/critical-care review; if not stabilising within **4 h**, consider PICU transfer/escalation. Reassess respiratory rate/effort, HR, SpO2/FiO2, mental state, perfusion and gas exchange.
- **CPAP/NIV:** use only where the child can be closely monitored and rapidly escalated. RCH states acute respiratory failure requiring CPAP/NIV should be managed in a PICU-capable pathway; initiation may occur in ED/PICU. Contraindications include respiratory arrest, unstable cardiorespiratory status, inability to protect the airway, uncooperative patient where interface cannot be safely used, and relevant facial/oral/oesophageal surgery/injury.
- **Do not import adult NIV settings:** pediatric CPAP/IPAP/EPAP are disease-, age-, interface- and device-specific. This module deliberately does not copy the adult IPAP 10--15 / EPAP 4 starting rule. Use a pediatric respiratory/PICU protocol and titrate to work of breathing, ventilation, oxygenation and comfort.
- **Bag-mask ventilation:** ERC/RCUK 2025 recommends BMV as first-line support for inadequate spontaneous ventilation. Use correct head position, mask size/seal and a two-person technique when difficult; provide enough pressure/time for visible but not excessive chest rise and avoid hyperinflation.
- **Ventilation rate reference during supported ventilation:** ERC/RCUK 2025 pragmatic rates are about **25/min in infants**, **20/min in children >1 y**, **15/min in children >8 y**, and **10/min in children >12 y**, adjusted to physiology and gas exchange.
- **Invasive ventilation after intubation:** in previously healthy children start around **6--8 mL/kg ideal body weight** tidal volume and **PEEP 5 cm H2O**, then individualise PEEP/FiO2 and respiratory rate to oxygenation/ventilation targets. Use continuous ETCO2, confirm PaCO2 relationship with blood gas and avoid both hypo- and hyperventilation.
- **Sudden deterioration on support:** use DOPES: displacement, obstruction, pneumothorax/pathology, equipment, stomach/stacking/sedation. Revert to bag-mask ventilation and troubleshoot immediately when device failure or airway problem is suspected.
- **Escalation triggers:** rising oxygen requirement, persistent severe retractions/grunting, apnea, fatigue, reduced respiratory drive, worsening hypercapnia/acidosis, haemodynamic instability or deteriorating mental state -> senior/PICU review and invasive airway planning.
## pediatric-iv-fluid-therapy

- Scope: pediatric IV-fluid selection, dose and timing beyond the neonatal transition. Use the syndrome-specific module in parallel. **Do not transfer a gastroenteritis, sepsis, DKA, hypernatraemia, haemorrhage or cardiogenic-shock fluid plan to another syndrome.**
- **Assess first:** measured weight, recent weight change when available, perfusion/shock, hydration severity, urine output, ongoing losses, sodium/potassium/glucose/urea/creatinine when IV fluids are needed, and cardiac/renal/neuro/DKA/sepsis/trauma context. Serial weights are the best measure of acute fluid change; clinical percentage estimates are approximate.
- **Fluid types:** for resuscitation use a **glucose-free isotonic crystalloid with sodium 131--154 mmol/L**. Examples include 0.9% sodium chloride and balanced solutions such as Hartmann/lactated Ringer or other balanced buffered crystalloids. For routine maintenance, start with an **isotonic crystalloid**; glucose-containing isotonic fluid is appropriate when glucose supply is needed. Add potassium only after potassium/renal function and urine output are known and replacement is indicated.
- **General resuscitation (NICE NG29):** **10 mL/kg IV/IO over <10 min**, then reassess immediately. Use smaller aliquots in cardiac/renal disease. If **40--60 mL/kg** or more is required during initial resuscitation, seek PICU/expert input and re-evaluate the shock phenotype rather than continuing reflex boluses.
- **Septic shock (SSC 2026):** when ICU capability is available, use **10--20 mL/kg per bolus**, reassessing after every bolus, up to **40--60 mL/kg during the first hour** if shock persists and no fluid overload develops. Prefer balanced/buffered crystalloid over 0.9% saline when appropriate; 0.9% saline remains acceptable and may be preferred with selected electrolyte abnormalities or concern for raised ICP. Start vasoactive therapy early when perfusion remains abnormal or further fluid is unsafe.
- **Cardiogenic shock:** fluid is not automatic. If preload responsiveness is plausible and no pulmonary/systemic congestion is present, use a small trial such as **5 mL/kg** with immediate reassessment; prioritize echo/phenotype and inotrope/vasoactive support when appropriate.
- **Haemorrhagic shock:** control bleeding and move early to blood products/whole blood. Keep crystalloid exposure minimal; the linked pediatric shock pathway uses **no more than 20 mL/kg crystalloid** before blood-product strategy takes precedence when major haemorrhage is suspected.
- **Gastroenteritis without shock:** use low-osmolarity ORS first. NICE CG84 uses **50 mL/kg over 4 h plus maintenance**, given frequently in small amounts; NG administration is appropriate if drinking fails but the gut remains usable.
- **Gastroenteritis with shock:** give **0.9% sodium chloride 10 mL/kg rapidly**, reassess, and if still shocked give another **10 mL/kg**. If shock persists after the second rapid infusion, reassess for another cause and obtain PICU input. When shock resolves, move from resuscitation to deficit + maintenance rather than continuing rapid boluses.
- **IV rehydration after gastroenteritis, not hypernatraemic:** use an isotonic solution such as **0.9% sodium chloride or 0.9% sodium chloride + 5% glucose**. If shock boluses were required, add **100 mL/kg deficit replacement** to maintenance; if the child was not shocked but requires IV rehydration, add **50 mL/kg deficit replacement** to maintenance. Reassess clinical response and labs; transition back to enteral rehydration as soon as tolerated.
- **Generic dehydration by estimated percentage:** approximate deficit as **% dehydration x 10 mL/kg** (for example 5% = 50 mL/kg; 10% = 100 mL/kg). RCH guidance advises that when dehydration is >5%, a practical general approach is to replace **up to the first 5% (50 mL/kg) during the first 24 h**, then replace the remaining deficit over the following 24 h, unless a syndrome-specific pathway requires a different pace. Do not use this generic schedule for shock, DKA or hypernatraemia.
- **Hypernatraemic dehydration:** after any immediate shock resuscitation, use **0.9% sodium chloride with glucose 5%** initially and replace the water deficit **over 48 h**, plus maintenance and ongoing losses. Target sodium fall **<=0.5 mmol/L/h and <=10--12 mmol/L/24 h**; check sodium frequently (NICE: every 4--6 h initially). If sodium fails to fall appropriately, reassess fluid composition rather than accelerating blindly.
- **Acute symptomatic hyponatraemia during IV therapy (NICE NG29):** consider **2.7% sodium chloride 2 mL/kg, max 100 mL, over 10--15 min**; repeat once if symptoms persist and consider a third bolus after checking sodium if still symptomatic. Measure sodium at least hourly during acute correction. Do not use fluid restriction alone for hyponatraemic encephalopathy.
- **DKA:** use the DKA pathway, not generic dehydration replacement. ISPAD supports **0.9% saline 10--20 mL/kg over 20--30 min** for volume depletion without shock; in the rare child with DKA and shock, **20 mL/kg 0.9% saline rapidly**, reassessing after each bolus. Replace estimated deficit plus maintenance over **24--48 h** using **0.45--0.9% saline or balanced crystalloid** according to sodium/osmolality/clinical status. Add glucose as glycaemia falls according to the DKA protocol; RCH uses 0.9% saline for at least the first 6 h and adds 5% glucose when BGL is <=15 mmol/L, escalating glucose concentration if needed while acidosis persists.
- **Routine maintenance:** Holliday-Segar **100/50/20 mL/kg/day**. Full-maintenance hourly approximation is **4/2/1 mL/kg/h**. NICE recommends isotonic crystalloid for routine maintenance. In many unwell children with non-osmotic ADH secretion, consider **50--80% of calculated maintenance** (RCH commonly uses about **2/3 maintenance**) rather than automatically giving 100%.
- **Ongoing abnormal losses:** replace measured losses separately from maintenance/deficit. NICE recommends **0.9% sodium chloride containing potassium** for ongoing-loss replacement when potassium is appropriate; reassess electrolytes and glucose and tailor to the composition of the loss.
- **Worked examples:** 12 kg shock bolus at 10 mL/kg = **120 mL over <10 min**. 18 kg clinical dehydration treated enterally = **900 mL ORS over 4 h plus maintenance**. 25 kg full maintenance = **1,600 mL/day = 66.7 mL/h**; 2/3 maintenance ~= **44.4 mL/h**. A 20 kg estimated 7% deficit = **1,400 mL total deficit**; generic non-special-syndrome replacement would give the first 1,000 mL (5%) across the first 24 h and the remaining 400 mL across the next 24 h, in addition to the individually adjusted maintenance/ongoing-loss plan.
- **Reassessment/stop rules:** after every resuscitation bolus reassess HR, BP, pulse quality, refill, mental state, urine output, liver edge, lung exam/work of breathing and signs of overload. Recalculate the plan after significant urine/GI/drain losses or weight change. Stop/escalate for pulmonary oedema, hepatomegaly/congestion, worsening respiratory status, falling urine output despite resuscitation, rapidly changing sodium, cerebral symptoms or persistent shock.


## pediatric-electrolyte-emergencies

- Scope: acute electrolyte/glucose emergencies in children beyond neonatal transition. Confirm age, weight, ECG, renal function, glucose and acid-base status; treat life-threatening physiology before waiting for a repeat laboratory sample when the clinical scenario is convincing.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** stop potassium sources, obtain continuous ECG monitoring for moderate/severe cases and repeat the sample when feasible without delaying treatment. A normal ECG does **not** exclude arrhythmia risk.
- **Membrane stabilization in life-threatening hyperkalaemia/ECG change:** calcium gluconate 10% **0.68 mL/kg (0.15 mmol/kg), max 30 mL** IV/IO; or calcium chloride 10% **0.2 mL/kg (0.14 mmol/kg), max 10 mL**. Give over **2--5 min if unstable** or **15--20 min if stable**, with continuous cardiac monitoring. Calcium does not lower serum potassium.
- **Shift potassium intracellularly:** severe hyperkalaemia: glucose 10% **5 mL/kg IV** plus short-acting insulin **0.1 unit/kg IV, max 10 units**; monitor glucose every 30--60 min. Nebulized salbutamol: **2.5 mg if <=25 kg**, **5 mg if >25 kg**; severe cases may require higher total doses under senior monitoring. Sodium bicarbonate is reserved for concurrent metabolic acidosis, not routine hyperkalaemia.
- **Hypokalaemia:** oral/enteral replacement is preferred when safe. Acute oral KCl **1--2 mmol/kg/dose, max 20 mmol/dose**; recheck before repeating, with a usual ceiling **5 mmol/kg/day, max 50 mmol/day** in the cited pediatric guideline.
- **IV potassium is high-risk:** use an infusion pump and ECG monitoring when indicated. General ward: **0.2 mmol/kg/h for 3 h, max 10 mmol/h**. Critical care: **0.4 mmol/kg/h for 1--2 h, max 20 mmol/h**, usually requiring central access/concentrated protocol. Never IV push potassium and never flush a potassium-containing line.
- **Hypomagnesaemia:** mild/asymptomatic -> oral/enteral magnesium where possible. RCH pediatric guidance: **0.1--0.2 mmol/kg PO three times daily**, increasing if needed/tolerated; severe symptomatic cases -> IV magnesium **0.1--0.2 mmol/kg, up to 0.4 mmol/kg, max 8 mmol**, generally over **2--4 h**, faster only for severe monitored emergencies.
- **Acute symptomatic hypocalcaemia (>28 days):** calcium gluconate 10% **0.3--0.6 mL/kg IV (0.07--0.13 mmol calcium/kg) over 5--10 min** with ECG monitoring. For a simple emergency reference, **0.5 mL/kg = about 0.11 mmol/kg** is within the licensed pediatric range. Avoid extravasation; use a large vein/central access when possible. Do not run calcium through the same line as ceftriaxone where precipitation risk applies.
- **Hypernatraemia:** route to `pediatric-iv-fluid-therapy`. Moderate hypernatraemic water deficit is typically corrected with 0.9% sodium chloride + glucose 5% over **48 h**, targeting sodium fall **<=0.5 mmol/L/h and <=10--12 mmol/L/24 h**. Severe hypernatraemia (>=170 mmol/L) needs expert/ICU input and may require slower correction.
- **Acute symptomatic hyponatraemia:** NICE pediatric IV-fluid guidance supports **2.7% sodium chloride 2 mL/kg, max 100 mL, over 10--15 min**; repeat once if symptoms persist and consider a third bolus after checking sodium if still symptomatic. Measure sodium at least hourly during acute correction. Do not treat hyponatraemic encephalopathy with fluid restriction alone.
- **Severe hypoglycaemia:** if enteral correction is unsafe/impossible, glucose 10% **2 mL/kg IV/IO**; recheck promptly and follow with slow-release carbohydrate or glucose-containing maintenance fluid while treating the cause. The pediatric status-epilepticus pathway retains its separate HSE 10% glucose 3 mL/kg rule and must not be silently merged.
- Always reassess after electrolyte intervention: ECG, neurologic state, repeat electrolytes/glucose, urine output and renal function. Check associated abnormalities (especially magnesium with potassium/calcium disturbances) and stop/slow replacement if values are correcting too rapidly or signs of overload/toxicity occur.

## pediatric-blood-transfusion-major-hemorrhage

- Scope: blood-product support for children with acute haemorrhage/trauma and weight-based component prescription. Separate **critical bleeding** from stable/non-bleeding transfusion; do not apply a routine slow transfusion rate to exsanguination.
- **Major haemorrhage:** control external/internal bleeding first, activate the major-haemorrhage pathway and involve surgery/interventional radiology/transfusion medicine early. ERC/RCUK 2025: minimise crystalloid in haemorrhagic shock (**maximum about 20 mL/kg**) and give blood products or whole blood as soon as available.
- **Initial trauma blood:** RCH trauma guidance uses packed red cells **10 mL/kg** when there is ongoing visible bleeding or suspected occult bleeding with inadequate circulation. Treat this as an early trauma reference, not a complete massive-transfusion pack.
- **TXA:** ERC/RCUK 2025: for trauma requiring transfusion or life-threatening haemorrhage, give **15--20 mg/kg IV, max 1 g over 10 min**, then **2 mg/kg/h, max 1 g** for at least **8 h or until bleeding stops**, as early as possible and within 3 h of injury. Do not duplicate a different TXA regimen from an older local major-haemorrhage document.
- **Component-pack boundary:** pediatric massive-haemorrhage packs and RBC:plasma:platelet ratios vary by system. Do not hard-code one institutional pack as universal. The system should calculate individual component volumes when a protocol requests them and preserve the protocol source.
- **Red cells outside uncontrolled critical bleeding:** for a non-bleeding child **<20 kg**, RCH uses: `RBC volume (mL) = weight (kg) x 0.5 x desired Hb rise (g/L)`, with a usual maximum Hb increment of **20 g/L**. Typical rate **5 mL/kg/h**, often starting slower for the first 15 min; complete within **4 h**. For >20 kg, one RBC unit then reassess rather than automatically scaling mL/kg indefinitely.
- **Platelets:** for children **<15 kg**, **10 mL/kg**. For >15 kg, RCH generally uses one platelet unit. Typical non-critical rate **10--20 mL/kg/h**. Platelet thresholds depend on bleeding severity: approximately **30 x10^9/L** for minimal bleeding, **50 x10^9/L** for moderate bleeding, **75 x10^9/L** for severe bleeding/major-haemorrhage protocol, and **100 x10^9/L** for intracranial or retinal haemorrhage.
- **Fresh frozen plasma:** **10--20 mL/kg** when there is clinically significant bleeding with coagulopathy or another supported indication. Typical non-critical rate **10--20 mL/kg/h**. Do not give FFP merely to correct a mildly abnormal INR/APTT in a non-bleeding child.
- **Cryoprecipitate:** **5--10 mL/kg**. Use in active bleeding/major haemorrhage when fibrinogen is **<1.5 g/L** or there is hyperfibrinolysis; typical non-critical rate **10--20 mL/kg/h**.
- **Critical bleeding rate:** during exsanguinating/critical bleeding, transfuse **as fast as clinically indicated** with blood warmer/rapid delivery appropriate to patient size and access; routine non-critical rates do not apply. All products should still be completed within the product/hospital maximum hang time.
- **Calcium/temperature/coagulation:** prevent hypothermia; monitor ionised calcium, blood gas, Hb, platelets, PT/INR/APTT and fibrinogen repeatedly. Correct hypocalcaemia using the pediatric electrolyte pathway. Do not wait for complete laboratory normalization before source control.
- **Targets are context dependent:** avoid treating laboratory thresholds in isolation. Intracranial bleeding, congenital heart disease, ECMO, oncology, neonates and chronic transfusion programmes may need different thresholds/products.
- **Special product safety:** irradiated/CMV-negative/phenotype-matched/HLA-matched products are indication-dependent. This module does not infer those requirements; use transfusion-medicine guidance.
- **Worked examples:** 12 kg trauma patient -> initial RBC reference **120 mL**. Stable 10 kg child with Hb 60 g/L and desired Hb 80 g/L -> `10 x 0.5 x 20 = 100 mL RBC`; at 5 mL/kg/h = **50 mL/h**, about 2 h. A 12 kg child requiring FFP 10--20 mL/kg -> **120--240 mL**. Cryoprecipitate 5--10 mL/kg -> **60--120 mL**.
## pediatric-emergency-medications

- Scope: cross-cutting pediatric emergency dose/volume support for children beyond the neonatal transition, generally **>=1 month to <18 years**. It does not choose the diagnosis or indication; combine it with the syndrome module (`pediatric-cardiac-arrest`, `pediatric-arrhythmias`, `pediatric-shock`, `pediatric-status-epilepticus`, `anaphylaxis`, etc.) plus `medication-selection-safety`.
- Use a measured weight in kg whenever possible; if an emergency estimate is unavoidable, document the method. Apply adult maximum doses where the source specifies them. RCUK/ERC 2025 specifically warns that many pediatric resuscitation drugs are hydrophilic and doses in obesity may need adjustment; use an established pediatric emergency drug chart and avoid exceeding adult caps.
- **Source hierarchy for pediatrics:** use ERC/RCUK 2025 as the primary European resuscitation pathway; cross-check AHA/AAP PALS 2025; use SSC pediatric sepsis 2026 for sepsis/shock, NICE for IV-fluid therapy, HSE 2025 for pediatric convulsive status, PANDEM 2022 for PICU pain/sedation/delirium, and official product information for formulation restrictions. When sources disagree, preserve the disagreement rather than blending algorithms.
- **Arithmetic engine:** `scripts/pediatric_emergency_calculator.py` performs deterministic calculations only. It never selects the drug, dose, concentration or route. For a weight-based bolus: `total dose = dose/kg x weight`, then cap at the source maximum. For volume: `mL = total dose / stock concentration`. For continuous infusion: `mL/h = dose/kg/min x weight x 60 / concentration` or `mL/h = dose/kg/h x weight / concentration`.
- **High-alert pediatric rule:** independently verify weight, units, decimal position, concentration, maximum dose, route and pump rate for vasoactives, insulin, anticoagulants/thrombolytics, opioids/sedatives, neuromuscular blockers and concentrated electrolytes. Do not infer a pediatric standard concentration unless it is explicitly supported by an official product label, current guideline, or recognized pediatric pharmacology source.
- **Propofol ICU-sedation evidence conflict:** PANDEM 2022 allows short-term continuous propofol (<4 mg/kg/h for <48 h) as a selected PICU strategy, while some European SmPCs prohibit ICU sedation in patients <=16 years. Preserve this as a guideline/product-label conflict; do not auto-generate a routine pediatric ICU propofol pump table without product/jurisdiction review.
- **Cardiac arrest (ERC/RCUK 2025):** adrenaline **10 micrograms/kg IV/IO, max 1 mg**; repeat according to the pediatric arrest algorithm. In shockable arrest give amiodarone **5 mg/kg, max 300 mg after the 3rd shock** and **5 mg/kg, max 150 mg after the 5th shock**. Lidocaine **1 mg/kg IV** is an alternative when the selected resuscitation guideline supports it. ERC/RCUK 2025 remains the primary European pathway; AHA/AAP PALS 2025 is tracked as an independent comparison and must not be silently merged where algorithms differ.
- **Pre-arrest bradycardia/poor perfusion:** consider adrenaline **1--2 micrograms/kg IV** as small boluses while correcting oxygenation/ventilation and arranging definitive treatment. Atropine for selected vagal/conduction causes: **20 micrograms/kg IV** with age-specific maximums defined in `pediatric-arrhythmias`.
- **SVT/tachyarrhythmia:** adenosine and cardioversion doses remain owned by `pediatric-arrhythmias`; this module may calculate the weight-based total/volume only after that dose is selected.
- **Shock fluids (ERC/RCUK 2025):** standard resuscitation bolus **10 mL/kg** isotonic crystalloid with reassessment after every bolus. Cardiogenic shock, when fluid is needed, use a more cautious reference such as **5 mL/kg**. In haemorrhagic shock minimize crystalloid and keep cumulative crystalloid to **no more than 20 mL/kg** while moving early to blood products and a current pediatric/trauma major-haemorrhage guideline.
- **Trauma TXA in children requiring transfusion/life-threatening haemorrhage (RCUK/ERC 2025):** loading **15--20 mg/kg IV, max 1 g over 10 min**, then **2 mg/kg/h, max 1 g** for at least 8 h or until bleeding stops. The infusion rate in mL/h requires a source-verified final concentration from official product information or a recognized pediatric pharmacology source.
- **Routine maintenance IV fluids (NICE NG29):** use Holliday-Segar daily maintenance: **100 mL/kg/day for the first 10 kg + 50 mL/kg/day for the next 10 kg + 20 mL/kg/day for each kg above 20 kg**; divide by 24 for mL/h. Initial routine maintenance should use an isotonic crystalloid; adjust for illness, ADH-mediated water retention, renal/cardiac disease and measured electrolytes/glucose rather than blindly applying the calculated rate.
- **Gastroenteritis IV deficit reference (NICE CG84):** when IV rehydration is required and hypernatraemia is absent, NICE uses **100 mL/kg deficit replacement if the child required shock boluses** or **50 mL/kg if not shocked**, added to maintenance with ongoing clinical/laboratory reassessment. This is syndrome-specific and must not be transferred to DKA, cardiac/renal failure or hypernatraemic dehydration.
- **Hypoglycaemia boundary:** ERC 2025 general PLS uses **0.2 g/kg IV glucose = 2 mL/kg of 10% glucose** when oral treatment is not possible. The HSE 2025 pediatric status-epilepticus pathway uses **10% glucose 3 mL/kg when glucose <2.6 mmol/L**. Do not merge these into one universal dose: when treating status epilepticus, follow `pediatric-status-epilepticus`; otherwise use the syndrome-specific current pediatric hypoglycaemia guidance.
- **Canonical pediatric bolus registry:** source-verified non-infusion emergency doses live in `qa/pediatric-bolus-medications.json`; `scripts/pediatric_bolus_calculator.py` computes weight-based dose and volume only when the registry contains an explicit source concentration. Current entries cover IM adrenaline for anaphylaxis, glucose/insulin/electrolytes, acute-asthma rescue, RSI, analgesia and opioid reversal.
- **Analgesia and home-dose layer:** paracetamol and ibuprofen are stored as dose-per-kg references without automatic syrup volume because marketed concentrations vary.
- **Discharge/home-use pediatric layer:** condition-specific entries now include salbutamol pMDI via spacer after an improving asthma exacerbation, single-dose dexamethasone for severe croup, and low-osmolarity ORS 50 mL/kg over 4 h for gastroenteritis with clinical dehydration. These are diagnosis-linked entries, not a generic home-medication list. Intranasal fentanyl includes a source concentration of 50 micrograms/mL and can therefore produce a source-based volume; monitor for opioid adverse effects and reassess pain.
- **Canonical pediatric antibiotic registry:** syndrome-specific source-verified entries live in `qa/pediatric-antibiotics.json`; `scripts/pediatric_antibiotic_calculator.py` performs dose arithmetic only after the syndrome-specific regimen is selected. It refuses to borrow a dose from another indication, and it does not convert to mL unless a source concentration is explicitly present.
- **Pediatric antibiotics:** do **not** build a universal weight-based antibiotic table. Route to the syndrome-specific pathway and current guideline (for example febrile infant, meningitis/CNS infection, sepsis, pneumonia, UTI, skin/soft tissue infection) and apply age, weight, renal function, allergy, local resistance and maximum-dose rules. Antibiotic volume may be calculated only after a specific drug, dose and product concentration have been selected from the relevant current source.
- **Canonical pediatric infusion registry:** source-verified continuous-infusion ranges, preparation modes, concentrations, restrictions and source notes live in `qa/pediatric-infusion-localization.json`. `scripts/pediatric_pump_table.py` is the only automatic mL/h table generator; it fails closed for insufficiently verified, off-label/product-restricted or out-of-range entries.
- **Worked arithmetic examples are not prescriptions:** 20 kg at 10 micrograms/kg adrenaline = 200 micrograms total; if and only if a verified preparation were 100 micrograms/mL, the arithmetic volume would be 2 mL. A 25 kg child has Holliday-Segar maintenance 1,600 mL/day = 66.7 mL/h. A 20 kg 10 mL/kg bolus = 200 mL. These examples exist to test arithmetic, not to define a universal stock/concentration.
- Stop and reassess whenever the calculated result conflicts with an adult maximum, product label, current pediatric drug reference, renal/hepatic limits, haemodynamic response or a syndrome-specific maximum. Neonates <1 month require a neonatal-specific pathway.

## obstetric-emergencies

- Stabilize the pregnant/postpartum patient first while assessing gestational age and fetal needs; involve obstetrics, anesthesia and neonatology early when relevant. Maternal resuscitation takes priority: do not withhold necessary imaging, defibrillation, drugs or procedures solely because of pregnancy.
- Major threats include ectopic pregnancy, obstetric haemorrhage, severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, sepsis, thromboembolism, placental abruption/accreta spectrum and uterine rupture. Postpartum disease remains possible after delivery.
- **Acute severe hypertension:** ACOG continues to define severe-range BP as **SBP >=160 mm Hg and/or DBP >=110 mm Hg**. If persistent (about **15 min**), treat urgently; established guidance targets treatment within 30--60 minutes of confirmation.
- First-line emergency options remain pregnancy-specific: IV labetalol **20 mg over 2 min**, then 40 mg after 10 min and 80 mg after another 10 min if still severe (usual algorithm maximum 220 mg); IV hydralazine **5--10 mg** with reassessment per local algorithm; or immediate-release nifedipine 10 mg PO, followed by 20 mg doses when still severe, using the local first-hour ceiling. **Never give nifedipine sublingually.**
- Choose antihypertensive around asthma/bradycardia/heart failure, maternal haemodynamics and fetal status. Magnesium sulfate prevents/treats eclamptic seizures; it is **not** the antihypertensive.
- **Eclampsia/seizure prophylaxis:** magnesium sulfate is first-line. A common IV regimen supported by major obstetric guidance is **4--6 g IV loading over ~20--30 min**, followed by **1--2 g/h** maintenance; local protocol may use a specific variant. For recurrent seizure despite magnesium, give an additional magnesium bolus per obstetric protocol and involve anesthesia/critical care.
- Monitor respiration, deep-tendon reflexes, urine output and renal function during magnesium therapy. With clinically significant magnesium toxicity/respiratory depression, stop magnesium, support ventilation and give **calcium gluconate 1 g IV (10 mL of 10%) slowly** as antidotal therapy.
- Severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia requires definitive obstetric planning; BP control and magnesium stabilize the mother but do not replace assessment for delivery. Use pregnancy-specific haemorrhage, sepsis, thromboembolism and resuscitation pathways after verification.
- Portugal/Azores operational release requires reconciliation with the local maternity/emergency algorithm, magnesium presentation/pump standard, antihypertensive stock and transfer/delivery pathway.

## geriatric-emergencies

- Establish baseline cognition/function, frailty, goals of care, medications, sensory needs and caregiver observations.
- Expect atypical presentation and multifactorial illness; screen for delirium, falls, infection, medication toxicity, dehydration, pain and occult injury.
- Use cautious but timely treatment, avoid age alone as a reason to undertreat, and plan disposition around function, support and risk of decompensation.

## delirium

- Diagnose acute fluctuating inattention/cognitive change with a validated bedside method appropriate to the setting and obtain collateral baseline; hypoactive delirium is easily missed. Search urgently for hypoxia, infection, glucose/electrolyte disorder, medication/toxin/withdrawal, urinary retention, constipation, pain and neurologic disease.
- Prioritize pain control, orientation, hydration, mobility, sensory aids, sleep and removal of triggers. Use an antipsychotic only for severe distress or immediate danger after de-escalation fails, at the lowest effective dose and shortest duration, with ECG/drug-risk and Parkinson/Lewy-body contraindication review; benzodiazepines are generally reserved for withdrawal or another specific indication.
- Avoid physical restraint when possible; reassess capacity, safeguarding and safe disposition.

## syncope-falls-frailty

- Separate syncope from seizure, mechanical fall and unexplained collapse. Obtain history/witness account, examination, 12-lead ECG and standing BP when safe; evaluate bleeding, structural/electrical heart disease, medication effect and neurologic findings.
- Do not order routine broad laboratory panels, EEG or head CT for uncomplicated syncope without findings that indicate them. Continuous monitoring and urgent cardiac assessment are warranted for high-risk ECG, exertional/supine syncope, persistent hypotension, major structural heart disease, family history of sudden death or serious injury.
- In older/frail patients, assess occult head/hip injury, anticoagulation, gait, cognition and home support.
- Do not discharge based on a risk score alone; integrate cause, recurrence risk, function and reliable follow-up.

## psychiatric-emergencies

- Exclude delirium, intoxication/withdrawal, neurologic disease, metabolic illness and trauma before attributing symptoms to primary psychiatric disease.
- Use verbal de-escalation and the least restrictive safe approach. If medication is required, match it to etiology and verify QT, respiratory, age and interaction risks.
- Assess capacity, violence/self-neglect risk, safeguarding and need for psychiatric or medical admission.

## suicide-risk

- Ask directly about thoughts, intent, plan, access to means, recent acts, substance use, psychosis, supports and protective factors.
- Treat medical consequences and maintain a safe environment with observation proportional to immediate clinical need. Do not use a risk scale, global low/medium/high label or protective-factor count alone to predict suicide, decide discharge or deny psychosocial assessment.
- Arrange timely psychosocial/specialist assessment, collaborative safety planning, lethal-means restriction and named follow-up. Do not use a no-suicide contract as disposition; confirm a safe transfer and provide explicit crisis/return instructions.

## febrile-infant-0-90-days

- Scope: infant ≤90 days with documented rectal temperature ≥38.0 °C at home or in care, or temperature <36.0 °C/ill appearance. This pathway is for a previously healthy, term, well-appearing infant only; prematurity, significant comorbidity, immunodeficiency, recent antibiotics, focal bacterial infection, or ill appearance excludes low-risk algorithms. Stabilize ill-appearing infants and activate neonatal/pediatric sepsis care immediately.
- Obtain age-appropriate history/exam and reliable temperature; collect urine for urinalysis and culture by catheter, suprapubic aspiration or reliable clean-catch (not a bag culture). A positive viral test does not cancel age-based bacterial evaluation. Blood/urine cultures and inflammatory markers (prefer procalcitonin when available) are used by age and validated local algorithm; do not use one biomarker or appearance alone to exclude invasive bacterial infection.
- 0–28 days: highest-risk group; urgent pediatric/neonatal assessment, blood/urine studies and inflammatory markers, with lumbar puncture, empiric antimicrobials and admission based on age, appearance and validated risk approach. A low-risk label does not make newborn outpatient care routine.
- 29–60 days: use a validated algorithm (e.g., PECARN or Step-by-Step) with urinalysis, urine culture, blood culture and inflammatory markers. LP, antibiotics and admission are guided by clinical status and marker risk; a positive urinalysis alone with reassuring markers does not automatically establish high IBI risk in well-appearing infants.
- 61–90 days: at minimum urine evaluation/culture; consider applying a validated 29–60-day pathway, with close follow-up if managed as outpatient. Ensure reassessment within 24–48 h and reliable caregiver access.
- Consider HSV in young infants with vesicles, seizures, ill appearance, CSF pleocytosis, thrombocytopenia or transaminitis; do not delay LP when neurologic signs are present. Route empiric antibiotic/acyclovir selection to `empiric-antibiotics` and neonatal/local guidance; verify age, weight, renal function, allergy and local resistance. Arrange admission/transfer if the necessary evaluation, monitoring or follow-up cannot be safely provided.
- Viral bronchiolitis/RSV lowers but does not eliminate bacterial risk. Fever in a young infant still follows the age-specific pathway; do not let a viral syndrome prematurely close the assessment.

## bronchiolitis

- Typical first episode of viral upper-respiratory symptoms followed by cough, tachypnea, crackles/wheeze and increased work of breathing in a child under 2 years (often <12 months). Assess apnea, exhaustion, cyanosis, feeding/hydration, perfusion and risk factors: age <3 months (especially <6 weeks), prematurity, chronic lung disease, hemodynamically significant congenital heart disease, neuromuscular disease or immunodeficiency.
- Clinical diagnosis. Do not routinely order chest radiograph, blood tests or viral PCR; test only if an alternative diagnosis/severe course/cohorting decision makes it useful. Consider bacterial pneumonia, sepsis, heart failure, foreign body or asthma when features are atypical.
- Supportive treatment: nasal saline/superficial suction only when secretions impair breathing or feeding; small frequent feeds or NG/IV hydration if intake inadequate. Give supplemental oxygen for persistent SpO₂ <90% in most infants aged ≥6 weeks without underlying disease, or <92% if age <6 weeks or underlying condition, following NICE persistent-hypoxemia thresholds and accounting for measurement quality and clinical state. Escalate respiratory support and PICU consultation for apnea, exhaustion, worsening hypoxemia or impending failure; route HFNO/CPAP/NIV to pediatric-acute-respiratory-support.
- Do not routinely give salbutamol, corticosteroids, antibiotics, nebulized hypertonic saline, chest physiotherapy, or epinephrine. If diagnosis is uncertain and a bronchodilator trial is contemplated for an alternate wheezing phenotype, document objective response and stop if ineffective; it is not routine bronchiolitis treatment.
- Admit/observe for apnea, significant work of breathing, persistent hypoxemia, inability to maintain hydration, very young age/high-risk condition, or unsafe home observation. Discharge only when work of breathing is manageable, oxygenation is stable on room air per age/risk threshold, feeding/urine output are adequate and caregivers can recognize apnea, cyanosis, worsening effort or dehydration.

## croup

- Viral upper-airway syndrome with barky cough, hoarseness and inspiratory stridor. Assess at rest, work of breathing, air entry, color, fatigue and mental status while minimizing distress; avoid unnecessary examination, IV access or imaging in a typical case.
- Red flags for alternate/critical diagnosis: drooling, dysphagia, toxic appearance/high fever, tripod posture, abrupt choking, neck stiffness, poor response to epinephrine, or progressive fatigue/silent airway. Consider epiglottitis, bacterial tracheitis, deep neck infection, foreign body or anaphylaxis; call anesthesia/ENT/PICU early for severe obstruction.
- Give one oral dexamethasone dose 0.6 mg/kg (maximum 12 mg per current RCH guidance); use IM if persistent vomiting or severe distress prevents oral administration. Lower doses (0.15–0.3 mg/kg) appear in studies but equivalence is less certain; use a consistent local protocol. Nebulized epinephrine for moderate/severe croup or stridor at rest: L-epinephrine 1 mg/mL, 5 mL nebulized (or racemic epinephrine 0.5 mL per formulation); this is a bridge, not definitive airway care.
- Reassess response and observe at least 3 h after nebulized epinephrine; discharge only if stridor/retractions do not recur, child drinks, no oxygen need, and caregivers have return precautions. Admit/escalate for persistent/recurrent stridor at rest, repeated epinephrine, hypoxemia, poor intake, exhaustion, atypical diagnosis or limited access to return care. Do not use humidified mist/heliox routinely or delay airway escalation.

## pediatric-dehydration-shock

- Assess mental state, heart rate, pulse quality, capillary refill, temperature/peripheries, work of breathing, blood pressure (hypotension is late), mucosa/tears, urine output, intake/losses and recent weight. Estimated dehydration by weight change is preferred when a reliable recent weight exists. Identify sodium/glucose risk, DKA, sepsis, hemorrhage, cardiac/renal disease and malnutrition; do not apply a gastroenteritis fluid plan to these syndromes.
- Mild/moderate dehydration without shock: oral rehydration solution in small, frequent amounts is first line; NG rehydration if oral intake fails and there is no contraindication. Continue breastfeeding/age-appropriate feeds and replace ongoing losses. Check glucose/electrolytes when severe dehydration, IV fluids, young infant/large losses, neurologic signs or comorbidity.
- Shock/poor perfusion: first classify likely cause; suspected cardiogenic shock is not a routine fluid-bolus pathway. ABC, monitor, obtain IV/IO access, glucose and cause-directed tests, senior pediatric support. Give glucose-free isotonic crystalloid 10 mL/kg IV/IO over <10 min, then reassess immediately (per NICE pediatric sepsis/IV-fluid guidance; NG254 caps each bolus at 250 mL in suspected sepsis). Repeat only if persistent shock and no overload, with reassessment after each bolus and early vasoactive/PICU escalation; use blood for hemorrhagic shock and smaller/individualized aliquots in cardiac/renal disease or malnutrition. Avoid unreviewed repeated large boluses.
- If perfusion normalizes, calculate deficit + maintenance + ongoing losses, choose oral/NG where possible, and use isotonic IV fluids with glucose/electrolytes guided by measured labs and local protocol. Hypernatremic dehydration requires controlled correction and serial sodium; DKA, burns, sepsis and neonatal fluid pathways take precedence over this generic module.
- Reassess vitals, perfusion, mental status, urine output, glucose and electrolytes after intervention; document response and escalation trigger. Admit/transfer for shock, severe dehydration/electrolyte disturbance, persistent vomiting, failed enteral rehydration, high-risk age/comorbidity or unsafe follow-up.

## pediatric-dka

- Scope: diabetic ketoacidosis in children/adolescents. Do not use the adult DKA/HHS fluid/bicarbonate pathway. Diagnostic triad: glucose >11 mmol/L, venous pH <7.3 or bicarbonate <18 mmol/L, and ketonaemia >=3 mmol/L or urine ketones >=2+.
- Start ABC monitoring, neurological observations, cardiac monitoring when potassium is abnormal, measured weight and frequent bedside glucose/electrolyte/ketone checks. Cerebral injury is the key life-threatening complication.
- Fluids: RCH 2025 starts with 0.9% sodium chloride. In a tachycardic child with delayed central capillary refill, give **10 mL/kg 0.9% sodium chloride over 30 min** and reassess. Rare shock requires cause-directed resuscitation and senior/PICU input; avoid reflex repeated rapid boluses.
- Potassium: use **40 mmol/L KCl** in rehydration fluid when serum K <=5.5 mmol/L and urine output is established; may increase to **60 mmol/L** if required. If K <3.0 mmol/L, replace potassium before insulin. Do not add potassium to a bag already hanging.
- Begin IV insulin only after **1 h of IV rehydration**. Do **not** give an insulin bolus. RCH: **0.1 units/kg/h** for most children; consider **0.05 units/kg/h** for age <5 y, inter-hospital transfer/limited biochemical monitoring, or BGL <15 mmol/L when insulin starts.
- Source-supported pump preparations: **1 unit/mL** (50 units in final 50 mL) or **0.1 unit/mL** (50 units in final 500 mL). The calculator reports mL/h for both concentrations; use one clearly labelled concentration and avoid decimal/concentration switching.
- Glucose: when BGL <=15 mmol/L, change to 0.9% sodium chloride with **5% glucose** plus potassium as indicated. If glucose falls <5 mmol/L or is dropping >5 mmol/L/h while acidosis persists, increase glucose to **10%** before reducing insulin. Aim BGL about 5--10 mmol/L while ketosis/acidosis resolves.
- Hypoglycaemia <4 mmol/L: give **10% glucose 2 mL/kg IV**, repeat if needed, and continue insulin while increasing glucose delivery. If glucose continues to fall despite 10% glucose, step insulin down (for example 0.1 -> 0.05 units/kg/h) under the pediatric DKA pathway.
- Corrected sodium (RCH): **corrected Na = measured Na + 0.4 x (glucose - 5.5)** with glucose in mmol/L. Track direction of corrected sodium and osmolality; discuss corrected Na <125 or >150 mmol/L early.
- Cerebral injury: do not wait for imaging. Elevate head, reduce fluid rate by about one-third and give either **mannitol 20% 0.5 g/kg IV over 20 min** or **3% sodium chloride 3 mL/kg IV over 15 min**; if no response within 15 min, escalate urgently to PICU/critical care.
- Bicarbonate is not routine. RCH restricts it to exceptional situations such as life-threatening hyperkalaemia or severe circulatory failure requiring inotropes, with intensive-care input.
- Transition to subcutaneous insulin only when alert, eating and metabolically stable (pH >7.3, bicarbonate >18 mmol/L); overlap rapid-acting subcutaneous insulin with the IV infusion for **30--60 min**.
- Use pediatric_dka_calculator.py for corrected sodium, insulin pump rates and emergency cerebral-injury doses. These arithmetic outputs do not replace serial biochemical/neurological reassessment.

## pediatric-burns

- Scope: acute thermal/chemical/electrical burns in children. Manage as trauma first; do not let burn appearance distract from airway, breathing, circulation, associated injury or non-accidental injury.
- First aid: cool the burn with **20 min of cool running water within 3 h of injury** when feasible. Remove hot/wet clothing and jewellery. Do not use ice. Prevent hypothermia, especially in infants/young children.
- Estimate burn size using a pediatric Lund-Browder chart. Do **not** include simple epidermal erythema in TBSA. Consider inhalation injury with facial burns, soot, hoarseness/stridor, enclosed-space exposure or respiratory distress; give high-concentration oxygen and plan early expert airway management when airway oedema is plausible.
- Burns **>=10% TBSA** require formal IV fluid resuscitation in children. RCH uses Hartmann solution for resuscitation and separate maintenance fluid (0.9% sodium chloride with 5% glucose). Calculate from the **time of burn**, not arrival time.
- Modified Parkland reference: **3 mL x weight (kg) x %TBSA** Hartmann over the first 24 h. Give **half in the first 8 h from injury** and the other half over the next 16 h. Maintenance fluid is additional in children using the **100/50/20 mL/kg/day** formula.
- If presentation is delayed during the first 8 h, the remaining first-half volume must be delivered over the time left until hour 8; do not restart a fresh 8-hour clock at hospital arrival. The calculated formula is only a starting estimate.
- Titrate resuscitation every 1--2 h to physiology and urine output rather than blindly following the formula. Target urine output about **1 mL/kg/h** in children. Electrical injury, inhalation injury and delayed resuscitation may increase requirements.
- Early shock immediately after a burn should prompt assessment for another cause (bleeding, trauma, sepsis, etc.); the burn itself does not explain early profound shock reliably.
- Provide early weight-based analgesia; procedural sedation may be required for debridement/dressings. Keep tetanus status current. Circumferential deep burns with impaired perfusion/ventilation require urgent burns/surgical assessment for possible escharotomy.
- Consult/transfer to a pediatric burns service for complex burns, including >5% TBSA, >5% full-thickness, special areas (face/eyes/ears/neck/hands/feet/genitalia/perineum/major joints), inhalation injury, circumferential, chemical/electrical, associated trauma, age <12 months or suspected non-accidental injury.
- Use pediatric_burn_calculator.py for Modified Parkland and maintenance arithmetic. Always adjust the calculated rate to urine output, perfusion, oedema and specialist burns guidance.

## pediatric-cns-infection

- Scope: suspected bacterial meningitis or encephalitis in children beyond the neonatal pathway. Obtain blood cultures and perform lumbar puncture before antimicrobials only when safe and when this does not materially delay treatment.
- Timing: RCH advises antibiotics within **30 min of the decision to treat**; NICE NG240 requires IV antibiotics **within 1 h of hospital arrival**. If LP is contraindicated or delayed >30 min, treat first. A normal CT does not exclude raised ICP.
- Age >=2 months: ceftriaxone **100 mg/kg IV every 24 h, max 4 g**, or cefotaxime **50 mg/kg IV every 6 h, max 2 g**. If pneumococcal resistance is a concern or Gram-positive cocci are seen, add vancomycin **15 mg/kg IV every 6 h** (RCH max 750 mg per dose).
- Age 1--2 months: use a young-infant regimen. RCH lists benzylpenicillin **60 mg/kg IV every 4 h** once >4 weeks plus cefotaxime **50 mg/kg IV every 6 h**; ceftriaxone **100 mg/kg IV every 24 h** may be used after 4 weeks only when product/age restrictions permit. Avoid ceftriaxone in premature infants <41 weeks corrected gestation and in neonates receiving calcium-containing IV solutions.
- Meningococcal disease: give a third-generation cephalosporin immediately; RCH uses ceftriaxone **100 mg/kg IV daily, max 4 g** when >4 weeks. Do not delay for investigations in an unstable child.
- Adjunct dexamethasone: for children >=2 months with presumed bacterial meningitis, RCH uses **0.15 mg/kg IV every 6 h, max 10 mg**, started just before or with the first antibiotic dose. Pediatric pathways differ on duration (for example 2 versus 4 days); preserve the source-specific duration rather than blending them.
- Possible HSV encephalitis: add aciclovir promptly. RCH: <3 months corrected age **20 mg/kg IV every 8 h** (>30 weeks gestation); age 3 months--12 years **500 mg/m2 IV every 8 h** or **20 mg/kg IV every 8 h**; >12 years **10 mg/kg IV every 8 h**. Adjust for renal function and ensure hydration.
- Manage shock, seizures, sodium/glucose abnormalities and raised-ICP physiology in parallel. Use droplet precautions when meningococcal/Hib disease is suspected until the applicable isolation period is complete.
- Reassess antimicrobials when CSF/PCR/cultures identify the organism. Typical pathogen-specific durations differ; do not use one universal duration.

## pediatric-toxicology

- Scope: accidental or deliberate poisoning in children/adolescents. Stabilize ABCDE, check bedside glucose, ECG when cardiotoxic ingestion is possible, identify substance/formulation/maximum possible dose per kg and time of ingestion, and contact a poison centre/clinical toxicologist early for significant or uncertain exposures.
- Decontamination: activated charcoal has a **very limited role** in children. RCH recommends toxicologist discussion and use only when ingestion is potentially severe, the child presents early (usually within **1--2 h**) and the airway is intact or protected. Do not use routine emesis or gastric lavage.
- Paracetamol: immediate-release single ingestion requires a timed concentration, typically at **4 h** after tablet ingestion. RCH recommends assessment for acute ingestion **>200 mg/kg or >10 g, whichever is less**. Do not use the nomogram for repeated supratherapeutic ingestion.
- Acetylcysteine when indicated: two-stage IV regimen totaling **300 mg/kg**: **200 mg/kg over 4 h** in total crystalloid volume **7 mL/kg, max 500 mL**, then **100 mg/kg over 16 h** in total volume **14 mL/kg, max 1000 mL**. For smaller children reduce carrier-fluid volume while delivering the full antidote dose.
- Tricyclic antidepressant poisoning: obtain serial ECGs. If QRS is widened or ventricular arrhythmia occurs, give sodium bicarbonate **2 mmol/kg IV** and repeat as required while targeting alkalinisation around pH 7.5 under toxicology/critical-care guidance.
- Opioid toxicity: prioritize ventilation. Use the source-verified pediatric naloxone entries in the pediatric bolus registry; titrate to restoration of adequate ventilation rather than full arousal.
- Treat seizures with benzodiazepines and use agent-specific antidote pathways rather than a generic antidote table. Whole-bowel irrigation, multidose charcoal, lipid emulsion, high-dose insulin, digoxin Fab, fomepizole and chelation require agent-specific evidence/toxicology input.
- Any deliberate self-poisoning in an adolescent requires psychosocial/self-harm assessment before discharge. Admit/transfer for abnormal mental state, respiratory depression, seizures, ECG abnormality, hemodynamic instability, significant metabolic disturbance or potentially delayed toxicity.

## pediatric-status-epilepticus

- Convulsive status: generalized convulsion >=5 min or recurrent seizures without recovery. Time seizure; ABC, lateral position/suction, oxygen/continuous SpO2 and cardiorespiratory monitoring, bedside glucose, temperature, IV/IO access without delaying first benzodiazepine. Pursue cause (febrile infection/meningitis, trauma, toxins, metabolic/electrolytes, missed antiseizure medication). If consciousness does not recover after motor seizure stops, consider nonconvulsive status/EEG.
- **HSE 2025 glucose gate:** if bedside glucose is **<2.6 mmol/L**, give **10% glucose 3 mL/kg** and recheck glucose in **10 min**, while continuing seizure management.
- If an individualized seizure plan exists, follow it. Count prehospital/home benzodiazepines: give no more than **2 total doses** to reduce respiratory depression. First-line options for age >=1 month:
  - buccal midazolam **0.3 mg/kg**; HSE dose bands: 1--3 months 0.3 mg/kg (max 2.5 mg), 3--11 months 2.5 mg, 1--4 y 5 mg, 5--9 y 7.5 mg, 10--17 y 10 mg;
  - IV lorazepam **0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg)**, diluted with an equal volume of 0.9% sodium chloride and given slowly;
  - rectal diazepam **0.5 mg/kg (max 20 mg)** when buccal/IV routes are unavailable, using the local formulation/dose bands.
  If still seizing 5 min after the first benzodiazepine, give the second and final benzodiazepine dose. Monitor ventilation and be ready to support the airway.
- If seizure persists after 2 benzodiazepine doses / around **15 min from onset**, urgently involve senior pediatrics, anesthesia/PICU and give IV levetiracetam **40 mg/kg (max 3 g)**, diluted to **50 mg/mL** and infused over **5 min**, following HSE 2025 and local drug policy. A loading dose may be given even if the child is already taking maintenance levetiracetam.
- Ongoing seizure after levetiracetam: HSE 2025 next step is IV phenytoin **20 mg/kg (max 2 g)** using a large vein with continuous ECG monitoring because of arrhythmia/extravasation risk; then phenobarbital **20 mg/kg (max 1 g)** with anesthesia/airway readiness. Local Portugal/Azores protocols may select a different equivalent second/third-line pathway and must take precedence once verified.
- **Consultant-neurology alternatives in HSE 2025:** lacosamide **10 mg/kg** may be used after specialist discussion; sodium valproate **20 mg/kg IV (max 3 g)** is another option. Avoid valproate in hepatic dysfunction, metabolic disease, pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, pregnancy, and in children <2 y with unknown etiology.
- Refractory status requires PICU/critical-care management, airway control when indicated and EEG-guided therapy. Do not stack repeated benzodiazepines or allow medication preparation/access attempts to delay time-critical treatment.
- This HSE pathway covers **1 month--18 years** and explicitly excludes neonates <1 month and children with established epilepsy who have increased seizures from baseline; use neonatal/individualized specialist pathways for those groups.
- Portugal/Azores operational release requires confirmation of local buccal midazolam/lorazepam/diazepam presentations, levetiracetam/phenytoin/phenobarbital concentrations and infusion standards, pediatric transfer/PICU pathway and EEG access.

## pediatric-shock

- Scope: infants, children and adolescents beyond newborn transition. Use pediatric ABCDE, measured/estimated weight in kg, age-specific vital signs and early PICU/resuscitation support. Hypotension is a late sign; assess perfusion using mental state, pulse quality, capillary refill, skin temperature/colour, urine output and trend, not BP alone.
- Classify the phenotype: hypovolaemic/haemorrhagic, distributive, cardiogenic, obstructive or dissociative/mixed. Treat the cause in parallel; POCUS/echocardiography and lactate may refine the phenotype but must not delay resuscitation.
- **Fluid bolus (ERC 2025):** for hypovolaemic, obstructive or distributive shock, give balanced isotonic crystalloid **10 mL/kg** and reassess after each bolus. If balanced crystalloid is unavailable, 0.9% saline is an alternative and may be preferred in DKA or severe traumatic brain injury. Reassess for crackles, increasing liver edge/JVP, worsening work of breathing or other overload after every bolus.
- Repeated 10 mL/kg boluses may be required; ERC 2025 notes that **40--60 mL/kg in the first hour** may be needed in hypovolaemic/distributive shock, but start vasoactive support early and do not continue fluids reflexively. In cardiogenic shock, individualize fluid and, when a bolus is needed, use a more cautious amount such as **5 mL/kg** with immediate reassessment.
- **Vasoactive timing:** start a continuous vasoactive infusion early according to shock phenotype, via a dedicated central or peripheral line when necessary, and **not later than after 3--4 boluses (30--40 mL/kg)**. Titrate to perfusion (pulse, refill, urine output, mental state, lactate/echo when available), not BP alone; aim for at least the age-specific 5th-centile systolic pressure.
- **Agent position:** ERC 2025 uses noradrenaline as first-line vasopressor, adrenaline as first-line inotrope, and milrinone as first-line inodilator according to shock phenotype. **For pediatric septic shock specifically, SSC 2026 found insufficient evidence to recommend epinephrine versus norepinephrine as the preferred first-line agent**; therefore do not present one universal septic-shock catecholamine as mandatory. Exact local concentrations and mL/h are deliberately deferred to the pediatric infusion/formulary module because decimal/concentration errors are high-risk.
- **Haemorrhagic shock:** control bleeding, activate the local major-haemorrhage protocol, minimize crystalloids (**max 20 mL/kg**) and give blood products/whole blood as soon as available. Do not use a crystalloid-only strategy when major haemorrhage is suspected.
- Seek expert advice early for refractory shock, congenital heart disease or potential extracorporeal support. Route to cause-specific modules as appropriate: `pediatric-dehydration-shock`, `anaphylaxis`, `trauma-major-hemorrhage`, `cardiac-tamponade-pericardial-emergency`, `tension-pneumothorax`, `adrenal-crisis`, or other syndrome-specific care.
- Newborn resuscitation at birth remains outside this module; use neonatal life support.

## pediatric-arrhythmias

- Confirm age/weight, pulse and perfusion; use pediatric ABCDE, monitor/defibrillator, vascular access and 12-lead ECG when this will not delay treatment. Correct hypoxia, ventilation failure, shock, fever, pain, electrolytes and toxins. Seek pediatric cardiology/resuscitation support early.
- **Bradycardia with poor perfusion:** optimize oxygenation/ventilation first. If the child remains bradycardic with poor perfusion and HR **<60/min despite adequate respiratory support**, start chest compressions. Consider adrenaline **1--2 micrograms/kg IV** as small boluses or continuous infusion. Atropine is reserved for selected vagal/conduction causes: **20 micrograms/kg IV** (up to age 11: max 0.5 mg; age 12--17: 300--600 micrograms, with larger emergency doses only per expert/local protocol). Pacing is uncommon and etiology-guided (e.g. complete heart block/sick sinus syndrome).
- **Stable SVT:** use vagal manoeuvres, then adenosine with ECG recording/defibrillator readiness. ERC/RCUK 2025: first dose **0.1--0.2 mg/kg IV** (max 6 mg); if SVT persists, **0.3 mg/kg IV** after at least 1 min (maximum 12--18 mg according to age/local chart). Recurrent or refractory SVT requires pediatric cardiology input rather than repeated empiric adult antiarrhythmics.
- **Unstable/decompensated tachyarrhythmia:** perform synchronized cardioversion without delay. Start at **1 J/kg** (ERC text permits 1--2 J/kg), then double the energy with each subsequent attempt up to **4 J/kg**. Use appropriate sedation/analgesia if conscious, but IV access attempts or sedation must not delay cardioversion. For persistent SVT/VT, consider **amiodarone 5 mg/kg by slow IV infusion (>20 min)** before the third cardioversion only with expert guidance.
- **Broad-complex tachycardia:** if uncertain whether VT or SVT, treat as VT. Avoid reflex AV-nodal blockade in irregular/pre-excited rhythms. Torsades de pointes: magnesium **25--50 mg/kg IV** (max 2 g) over 10--15 min, repeat once if necessary per ERC/RCUK 2025.
- If the pulse is lost, switch immediately to `pediatric-cardiac-arrest`. Do not use adult energy or fixed adult drug doses without pediatric weight-based conversion and maximum-dose checks. Newborn resuscitation at birth is excluded.

## pediatric-cardiac-arrest

- Scope: infants/children/adolescents beyond newborn transition. Recognize arrest clinically or from monitored loss of vital signs, call the resuscitation team, start high-quality CPR and ventilation, and attach self-adhesive defibrillator pads as soon as possible. In a child who becomes bradycardic **<60/min with poor perfusion despite adequate respiratory support**, start chest compressions even if a pulse is still detectable.
- Distinguish shockable (VF/pulseless VT) from non-shockable rhythms (PEA/asystole or severe bradycardia with poor perfusion). Use 2-min CPR cycles and keep rhythm/pulse checks under 5 s.
- **Non-shockable arrest:** obtain IV/IO access and give adrenaline **10 micrograms/kg IV/IO (max 1 mg) as soon as possible**, then repeat **every 4 min** (every other 2-min cycle) unless guided by invasive haemodynamics.
- **Shockable VF/pVT:** give a single shock **4 J/kg** (do not exceed the adult device-dependent ceiling, usually 120--200 J for standard initial shocks), immediately resume CPR for 2 min, and repeat the 4 J/kg shock strategy while the rhythm remains shockable. For refractory VF/pVT requiring >5 shocks, ERC 2025 allows stepwise escalation up to **8 J/kg (max 360 J)**.
- **After the 3rd shock:** give adrenaline **10 micrograms/kg (max 1 mg)** plus amiodarone **5 mg/kg (max 300 mg)** IV/IO. **After the 5th shock:** give another adrenaline dose plus amiodarone **5 mg/kg (max 150 mg)**. Lidocaine **1 mg/kg IV** is an alternative if amiodarone is unavailable or local policy uses lidocaine.
- A witnessed, ECG-monitored shockable arrest in a child already attached to a defibrillator may use up to **three stacked shocks** with standard weight-based energy, then resume CPR; if shockable rhythm persists, the special monitored sequence gives adrenaline/amiodarone after the 5th shock and a second amiodarone after the 7th shock. Do not apply this stacked-shock exception to routine unwitnessed arrest.
- Use 100% oxygen during CPR, effective bag-mask ventilation and an advanced airway only when the team can place it without disrupting compressions. Use waveform capnography when an advanced airway is in place. POCUS only during planned pauses and never to prolong the pause.
- Search and treat reversible causes: hypoxia, hypovolaemia/haemorrhage, hyper-/hypokalaemia and other metabolic causes, hypothermia, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins and pulmonary/coronary thrombosis. Major haemorrhage requires early blood products rather than repeated crystalloid.
- After ROSC, transition immediately to pediatric post-resuscitation care: controlled oxygenation/ventilation, haemodynamic support, glucose/temperature/seizure management and PICU/cause-directed evaluation. Newborn resuscitation immediately after birth follows neonatal life support.
