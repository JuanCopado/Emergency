# Renal, metabolic and toxicology modules

Shared gate: obtain glucose, ECG, electrolytes, acid-base status, renal function,
medications/toxins and volume/perfusion assessment. Repeat critical values and
verify whether results are affected by sampling error.

## electrolytes

- Adult assessment/routing framework: evaluate neurological symptoms, weakness, seizures, arrhythmia, ECG, renal function, urine output, acid-base status, volume state and causative medicines. Treat symptomatic or ECG-threatening abnormalities before complete diagnostic workup; confirm unexpected results without delaying life-saving therapy.
- Check sampling quality, hemolysis, units and the laboratory reference interval. If pseudohyperkalemia is plausible, obtain a properly collected repeat specimen and laboratory advice; do not assume every high result is artifactual or that a normal ECG excludes dangerous hyperkalemia. Interpret total versus ionized calcium with albumin/pH context and distinguish mmol, mEq and elemental versus salt mass.
- Route the named ion to `sodium-emergencies`, `potassium-emergencies` or `calcium-magnesium-emergencies`; do not use a generic replacement plan. Sodium needs acuity/symptoms, explicit correction limits, frequent measurements and a rescue plan for overcorrection. Potassium care distinguishes myocardial protection, intracellular shift and actual removal, with glucose surveillance after insulin. Calcium/magnesium salts and concentrations are not interchangeable by equal volume.
- Before replacement/infusion verify the actual product, concentration, compatible diluent, final volume, route/access, administration rate and monitoring with `medication-selection-safety`. Adjust for renal impairment, ongoing losses and interacting drugs; reassess magnesium in refractory potassium disorders. Never give concentrated potassium as an IV push or invent a pump rate from an unspecified concentration.
- Repeat critical values and ECG/physiology according to severity, treatment phase and the specific protocol. Monitor for rebound, hypoglycemia, fluid overload and treatment-related arrhythmia; persistent severe abnormalities or inability to monitor require senior/nephrology/critical-care escalation.
- GREEN here covers the assessment and routing framework only. It does not approve an unresolved sodium preparation, change an ion-specific evidence status or enable blocked medication contracts. Use pediatric-specific modules for children and document response and the handover/monitoring plan.

## sodium-emergencies

- **Scope and current status14/11/2026:** adult sodium emergencies. **GREEN clinical reference; nursing-prepared NaCl3% is explicitly reconfirmed as available by the owner.** Use the pediatric electrolyte pathway for children. Product/concentration, local-protocol and monitoring confirmations remain mandatory before administration; GREEN is not a pharmacy release or a pump-order authorization.
- Confirm glucose, measured serum osmolality, volume status, urine osmolality, urine sodium, urine potassium, hourly urine volume, renal function, medications and relevant adrenal/thyroid context. Distinguish true hypotonic hyponatraemia from hyperglycaemic/translocational hyponatraemia and pseudohyponatraemia before applying a correction algorithm.
- Calculate serum osmolality and effective tonicity when inputs are available. With mmol/L units: `calculated osmolality = 2 x serum Na + glucose + urea`; `effective tonicity = 2 x serum Na + glucose`. With mg/dL units use `2 x serum Na + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8`. Calculate `osmolal gap = measured osmolality - calculated osmolality`. Urine osmolality and urine sodium are measured values, not derivable from serum sodium alone.
- **Hypernatraemia urine assessment:** interpret urine osmolality with urine volume: >800 mOsm/kg usually indicates appropriate concentration; <300 mOsm/kg with polyuria suggests diabetes insipidus; 300--800 mOsm/kg is indeterminate and includes partial DI, osmotic diuresis and renal dysfunction. Interpret urine sodium in context of volume status, diuretics and kidney disease.
- When urine sodium, urine potassium and hourly urine volume are supplied, calculate electrolyte-free water clearance: `CeH2O = urine volume x [1 - (urine Na + urine K) / serum Na]`. Add stool/insensible losses separately. Use `scripts/sodium_water_balance.py` for deterministic arithmetic.
- **Severe or moderately severe neurologic symptoms attributable to hyponatraemia:** The published Society for Endocrinology 2022 approach supports **150 mL sodium chloride 3% IV over 20 min**, then reassess symptoms and sodium. Repeat boluses according to the current protocol until clinical improvement or approximately a **5 mmol/L initial rise** is achieved. Treat the symptomatic emergency, not the sodium number in isolation.
- After the initial response, stop routine hypertonic boluses and prevent overcorrection. Avoid a rise greater than 10 mmol/L in the first 24 hours and greater than 8 mmol/L in each subsequent 24 hours; use a stricter ceiling (generally <=8 mmol/L/24 h) when osmotic-demyelination risk is high (e.g. very low sodium, alcoholism, malnutrition, advanced liver disease or hypokalaemia). Check sodium after each emergency bolus and at least every 4 h during ongoing hypertonic treatment; shorten to about every 2 h with a sudden water diuresis (for example urine output >100 mL/h), unexpected acceleration or rescue. Track urine output and the cumulative rolling 24 h change; a ceiling is not a target. Potassium replacement can contribute to sodium correction.
- **Specialist relowering strategy:** if sodium is rising too fast, stop active sodium-raising therapy and obtain urgent endocrine/renal/critical-care input. Published European 2014 rescue references include IV glucose 5% (electrolyte-free water) 10 mL/kg over 1 h, with strict Na/urine/fluid monitoring, and consideration of desmopressin 2 micrograms IV, not repeated more frequently than every 8 h. Select and adjust to the actual overshoot, water diuresis, ODS risk and product; do not use a fixed desmopressin schedule blindly. The glucose reference is a one-hour intervention: volume = 10 x weight(kg) mL, pump rate = 10 x weight(kg) mL/h for that hour, then reassess. It is not an unrestricted maintenance infusion.
- **Hypernatraemia:** restore shock first with appropriate isotonic crystalloid, then replace free-water deficit enterally or with IV electrolyte-free water while accounting for ongoing losses. Determine whether the disorder is acute versus chronic/unknown before selecting a correction rate. For chronic/unknown-duration adult hypernatraemia, a conventional cautious starting limit is about 0.5 mmol/L/h and 10--12 mmol/L/day, with repeated Na checks and replacement adjusted to ongoing losses. The evidence base is limited and partly extrapolated from children; adult observational evidence does not establish a universal safe maximum or prove benefit from very slow correction. Acute sodium loading needs specialist selection of a different plan. Do not routinely re-raise sodium solely because a hypernatraemia correction target was exceeded; this differs from hyponatraemia relowering rescue.
- Explicitly document: symptoms, acuity, target sodium change, current sodium trajectory, urine output, ongoing losses, and stop/relowering thresholds.
- **Local hypertonic-saline preparation, owner report 14/11/2026:** nursing staff use NaCl 20% with sodium chloride 0.9%, withdrawing and replacing equal volumes. For a verified actual final volume of 100 mL: 89 mL 0.9% + 11 mL 20%; 250 mL: 222.5 mL + 27.5 mL; 500 mL: 445 mL + 55 mL. Each yields **3.001% w/v (approximately 3%)**. Exact arithmetic: `V20 = Vfinal x (3 - 0.9) / (20 - 0.9)`; the0.11 multiplier is rounded. These mixtures require a controlled actual final volume; the nominal bag volume alone does not verify it. Never simply add concentrate to a full bag without the stated withdrawal. Confirm the exact product/container, access, mixing/asepsis and independent high-alert checks; prepare for immediate use unless the exact validated procedure permits otherwise. The retrieved foreign20% concentrate label restricts peripheral mixtures to800 mOsm/L, below theoretical3% NaCl osmolarity (about1027 mOsm/L); reconcile the actual product/local emergency access protocol rather than declaring universal peripheral compatibility. The prescribed bolus volume/time remains separate from the prepared bag volume and from weight-based ICP rescue; this preparation reference does not authorize automatic bolus repetition.
- **2026 update boundary:** ESE/ERA lists a 2026 guideline project and an official PDF is marked draft/for review. A final published replacement was not verified in this review. Keep that candidate in the update queue; do not silently replace the published 2014/SfE 2022 emergency pathway or call the draft the final2026 guideline. Product-specific preparation readiness is recorded separately below; the reviewed clinical monitoring/rescue reference is retained.
- **Bolus administration and reassessment:**150mL over20min corresponds to450mL/h for20min, then stop/reassess. This pump rate is not a continuous450mL/h order. A100mL preparation cannot supply a150mL bolus; preserve the prescribed total volume/time when selecting verified containers and account for line dead space under the local procedure. Do not increase concentration to fit the bolus into a smaller container. Continuing symptoms after an initial~5mmol/L rise require senior reassessment, including other causes and explicit stop/correction limits, rather than repeated blind boluses.
- **Published safety implementation (SfE2022):** maintain a consistent sodium analytical platform during correction when feasible; direct point-of-care and dilution-based laboratory measurements are not automatically interchangeable. Senior supervision and close clinical/biochemical monitoring are required. Do not use vaptans for this severe/moderately severe symptomatic emergency, or the Adrogue-Madias prediction alone to set the amount/rate of emergency hypertonic treatment. Neither an equation nor an initial normal vital sign predicts the effect of emerging water diuresis.
- **Adult hypernatraemia evidence2026:** a MIMIC-IV target-trial emulation (published online2025, print2026; PMID41240509) associated faster early correction with better outcomes; this was not a randomized treatment trial. The6November2026 meta-analysis (PMID42989584) found no significant overall mortality association and substantial heterogeneity, with setting-dependent signals. These observations do not establish a universal faster target, validate pediatric extrapolation or mandate re-raising sodium after an exceeded adult hypernatraemia target. Individualize treatment to duration, cause, neurological state, ongoing losses and serial sodium; avoid both unmonitored rapid change and ineffective prolonged undercorrection.

Glucose5%,NaCl0.9% andNaCl0.45% are also available according to the owner. Keep these declarations and the accepted recipe; do not request them again. Availability does not establish interchangeability or a new dilution recipe.


## potassium-emergencies

- Obtain an immediate 12-lead ECG and continuous monitoring for severe potassium disturbance. Repeat an unexpected non-urgent value from a non-hemolyzed sample, but do not delay treatment for severe hyperkalemia, compatible ECG change, weakness or peri-arrest physiology.
- For hyperkalemic ECG toxicity outside cardiac arrest, give calcium gluconate 10% 30 mL IV over 10 minutes; in cardiac arrest/peri-arrest, give calcium chloride 10% 10 mL IV over 5 minutes. Reassess the ECG and repeat calcium according to response/protocol. Calcium stabilizes myocardium but does not lower potassium. UKKA July 2026 retains these doses/settings but downgrades the calcium dose recommendation to 2C; do not portray its evidence as high certainty.
- For severe hyperkalemia (K at least 6.5 mmol/L), use the UKKA July 2026 hospital algorithm: soluble insulin 10 units IV plus glucose 25 g by infusion over 15--30 minutes; consider the same regimen for K 6.0--6.4 according to context. The separate Appendix 4b describes a 5--15 minute large-vein injection specifically in 50 mL glucose 50%; do not generalize that injection timing to all glucose formulations or silently switch delivery methods. Use the hospital infusion algorithm as this module's default and verify local preparation/access. State the available glucose concentration and volume: 50 mL of 50%, 125 mL of 20%, or 250 mL of 10% each provides 25 g. If pretreatment glucose is below 7 mmol/L, UKKA recommends glucose 10% at 50 mL/h for 5 hours after the initial regimen. Check glucose at baseline and 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240, 300 and 360 minutes after insulin-glucose; monitor longer when clinical risk requires. Check potassium at least at 1, 2, 4, 6 and 24 hours after identification/treatment to detect rebound.
- Add nebulized salbutamol 10--20 mg for moderate/severe hyperkalemia, but never use it as monotherapy. Remove potassium with dialysis when indicated; consider sodium zirconium cyclosilicate for acute severe hyperkalemia and a current potassium binder according to the verified local pathway. Binders do not replace immediate cardioprotection, shifting therapy or indicated dialysis. Do not routinely use calcium resonium; bicarbonate is not routine unless a separate acid-base indication exists.
- For hypokalemia, identify symptoms, ECG change, magnesium deficiency, renal function and ongoing losses. Prefer oral replacement when safe. For IV potassium, use a ready-prepared solution when possible; a usual monitored rate is 10 mmol/h and a short emergency rate up to 20 mmol/h requires continuous ECG, a controlled pump and protocol-approved concentration/access. Never IV-push potassium and never write potassium only in mL without mmol and final concentration.

## calcium-magnesium-emergencies

- **Adult reference scope:** interpret calcium using ionized calcium when available, with albumin/pH context for total calcium; obtain magnesium, potassium, phosphate, renal function, ECG/QTc and causative drugs. Treat tetany, seizure, laryngospasm, hypotension or dysrhythmia promptly. Hypomagnesemia may prevent correction of hypocalcemia/hypokalemia; correct the associated deficit. Pediatric, obstetric and arrest dosing follow their dedicated pathways.
- **Severe/symptomatic hypocalcaemia:** endocrine emergency guidance supports **10--20 mL of 10% calcium gluconate IV**, diluted in **50--100 mL** glucose 5% or sodium chloride 0.9%, over about **10 min** with ECG monitoring; repeat to clinical/ionized-calcium response, then use a verified infusion if required. State final volume explicitly: a calculated adult intermittent example is 10 mL drug + 90 mL compatible diluent = final 100 mL over 10 min, after verifying the product's elemental-calcium content. Do not use this hypocalcemia dose as an automatic hyperkalemia dose.
- **Elemental-calcium equivalence is product-specific:** the common resuscitation reference **10 mL 10% calcium chloride ≈ 6.8 mmol Ca2+** assumes the dihydrate formulation; **10 mL 10% calcium gluconate ≈ 2.26 mmol Ca2+** is also an approximation, not a universal product identity. The retrieved CIMA calcium-chloride hexahydrate 100 mg/mL label (49072) states 9.13 mEq per 10 mL: 9.13 / 2 = **4.565 mmol**, not 6.8. The retrieved gluconate product (69465) states **2.25 mmol total calcium per 10 mL**, including its calcium-containing excipient. Never substitute equal volumes or use a generic '10%' shortcut; calculate volume = prescribed elemental mmol / verified mmol per mL. Chloride is more injurious on extravasation; use the product/access protocol, preferably central access when indicated.
- **Torsades with a pulse:** use the RCUK adult tachyarrhythmia algorithm, V3 March 2026: magnesium **8 mmol IV over 10 min** (approximately 2 g magnesium sulfate heptahydrate), with ECG/BP monitoring and expert reassessment. Correct triggers and associated potassium deficit; avoid QT-prolonging drugs, including amiodarone for long-QT torsades. Unstable sustained polymorphic VT requires immediate unsynchronized shock; pulseless VT follows the arrest pathway. Magnesium must not delay electrical treatment and is not routine treatment for every cardiac arrest.
- **Severe symptomatic hypomagnesaemia outside arrest:** ERC special-circumstances guidance uses magnesium sulfate **2 g (about 8 mmol) IV over 15 min**, with clinical/laboratory reassessment before further replacement. This emergency loading schedule is distinct from slower ordinary deficit replacement and from eclampsia/asthma regimens. ERC's special-circumstances table also lists a faster 1--2 min torsades schedule; this module deliberately selects the syndrome-specific RCUK March 2026 ten-minute schedule for perfusing patients, consistent with ERC ALS and the retrieved product label. Do not merge the two schedules.
- **Magnesium preparation example, not assumed local stock:** verified heptahydrate 500 mg/mL (CIMA 89749): 2 g / 500 mg/mL = **4 mL**, made up with 46 mL sodium chloride 0.9% or glucose 5% to a **final 50 mL** (40 mg/mL), given over the indication-specific time above. Its label rounds 1 g to 4.1 mmol, so 2 g is approximately 8.2 mmol; distinguish the resuscitation approximation from an exact mmol prescription. The 150 mg/mL heptahydrate product (23622) would require 2000 / 150 = **13.33 mL**, not 4 mL. Verify salt/hydration, strength, dose units and final volume before administration.
- **Monitoring and stops:** monitor ECG, BP, respiratory status, consciousness, tendon reflexes, urine output, Mg/Ca/K and renal function during repeated magnesium/infusion. Renal impairment requires individualized reduction and close levels; do not use routine repeated loading in severe renal failure. Stop for hypotension, bradycardia, respiratory depression, absent reflexes or magnesium toxicity; have calcium rescue and ventilation available. Monitor IV sites for calcium extravasation and stop the infusion immediately if suspected.
- **Symptomatic hypermagnesaemia:** stop magnesium, support airway/ventilation/circulation, and give calcium gluconate 10% **10--20 mL IV slowly with ECG monitoring**, verifying elemental content and response. Calcium antagonizes toxicity but does not remove magnesium. Obtain urgent renal/dialysis input for severe toxicity or impaired clearance.
- **Severe hypercalcaemia:** restore volume with isotonic crystalloid without causing overload; loop diuretics are **not routine calcium-lowering therapy**, reserved for overload. Treat the cause and select antiresorptive/calcitonin therapy with specialist input and renal assessment; avoid routine corticosteroids for every etiology.
- **Infusion compatibility/stability:** do not mix calcium with bicarbonate, phosphate or sulfate-containing solutions, including magnesium sulfate; use separate compatible administration or a verified flush sequence. Calcium and ceftriaxone must not be mixed or infused simultaneously; in patients >28 days, sequential administration requires label-compliant line replacement/flush and clinical checks. Neonatal restrictions are stricter. The retrieved calcium and magnesium labels require immediate use after opening/dilution; do not infer a universal 24 h room-temperature hang time or Y-site compatibility from chemical stability alone.
- For any continuous infusion, document salt/product, total amount, compatible diluent, final volume, concentration in elemental mmol/mL, access and pump rate: **mL/h = prescribed mmol/h / verified mmol/mL**. The emergency doses above do not authorize an unspecified maintenance infusion. Owner accepts this reviewed reference; exact dispensed-product/RCM and independent high-alert checks remain mandatory. No local brand, Pharmacy signature or automatic calculator clearance is inferred.

## toxicology

### Entrada y seguridad
- Estabilizar **ABCDE, glucemia, temperatura, convulsiones y ECG** antes de perseguir el tóxico. Registrar agente/formulación, dosis estimada, hora, vía, coingestas, liberación prolongada, función renal/hepática, embarazo y medicación habitual. En exposición desconocida buscar toxidrome, QRS/QTc, gasometría, anion gap/osmol gap, lactato y niveles dirigidos que cambien conducta.
- El marco de reanimación toxicológica se contrasta con AHA2025 (no sólo la actualización2023); las fichas de producto y referencias del centro toxicológico delimitan dosis adultas. Contactar precozmente CIAV/centro toxicológico o toxicólogo en toxicidad grave, agente desconocido, formulación retardada, indicación de antídoto, posible depuración extracorpórea, recurrencia o intoxicación pediátrica. En parada tóxica aplicar ALS y tratar simultáneamente la causa; puede requerirse reanimación prolongada, ECTR o VA-ECMO.
- **Carbón activado no rutinario**: considerar dosis única tras ingesta potencialmente tóxica de sustancia adsorbible, sobre todo precozmente, solo con vía aérea segura. No usar por reflejo en cáusticos, hidrocarburos de alta aspiración, metales/alcoholes no adsorbibles o cuando el riesgo de aspiración supera el beneficio.
- Ninguna tabla sustituye la verificación del producto local, concentración, peso, función renal/hepática ni consulta toxicológica. Antivenenos, antitoxinas y quelantes son especialmente producto/protocolo dependientes.

### Drogas recreativas y toxidromes
- **Cocaína / crack:** no existe antídoto específico. Para agitación, hipertermia, hipertensión, taquicardia o convulsiones usar benzodiacepinas tituladas y enfriamiento activo. Tratar síndrome coronario según fisiología; si QRS ancho/arritmia por bloqueo de sodio, **bicarbonato sódico 1–2 mEq/kg IV**, repetir guiado por QRS, perfusión y gasometría. Evitar beta-bloqueo aislado no titulado en toxicidad aguda grave; seleccionar control cardiovascular con toxicología/cardiología.
- **Anfetamina, metanfetamina, MDMA, mefedrona y otras catinonas sintéticas:** sin antídoto específico. Benzodiacepinas, enfriamiento rápido en hipertermia, fluidos/electrolitos según estado, tratar hiponatremia, rabdomiólisis, convulsiones y fallo multiorgánico. No normalizar sodio rápidamente por protocolo genérico.
- **Opioides (heroína, fentanilo y análogos, metadona, oxicodona, etc.):** soporte ventilatorio primero. **Naloxona** titulada para recuperar ventilación eficaz; en dependencia conocida pueden emplearse incrementos IV pequeños (p. ej. 0.04–0.4 mg), repitiendo/escalando según respuesta; vías IN/IM son alternativas cuando no hay IV. Si recurre tras opioide de larga duración, considerar perfusión de naloxona basada en la dosis efectiva previa bajo protocolo local.
- **Benzodiacepinas / Z-drugs:** soporte. **Flumazenilo** solo en casos muy seleccionados (iatrogenia o exposición aislada en paciente no dependiente, sin proconvulsivantes/QRS ancho); referencia adulta: 0.2 mg IV lenta, repetir 0.2 mg aproximadamente cada minuto hasta respuesta, habitualmente máximo 1 mg inicial. Evitar en sobredosis mixta, dependencia, epilepsia o riesgo de abstinencia/convulsión.
- **GHB/GBL:** sin antídoto específico; vía aérea/ventilación y vigilancia. La recuperación puede ser brusca.
- **Cannabis y cannabinoides sintéticos:** sin antídoto; soporte, benzodiacepina si agitación/convulsión, evaluar isquemia, arritmia, lesión renal y coingestas en sintéticos.
- **PCP, ketamina, LSD y alucinógenos:** sin antídoto específico; ambiente controlado, benzodiacepinas para agitación/convulsiones, enfriamiento si hipertermia y tratamiento de trauma/rabdomiólisis.
- **Serotoninérgicos (ISRS/IRSN/IMAO, MDMA, tramadol, linezolid y combinaciones):** retirar agentes, benzodiacepinas, enfriamiento y soporte. En síndrome serotoninérgico moderado-grave considerar **ciproheptadina 12 mg VO/NG**, luego 2–4 mg cada 2 h hasta respuesta, con **máximo 32 mg/24 h incluyendo la carga**, según la referencia adulta CAPQ actualizada 2026. Si persiste más de 24 h, acordar continuación con toxicología. No existe formulación IV.
- **Anticolinérgicos (antihistamínicos, tricíclicos, atropínicos, plantas):** soporte, benzodiacepinas. **Fisostigmina** puede considerarse para delirium antimuscarínico puro tras ECG y consulta toxicológica; referencia adulta CAPQ: 0.5 mg IV lentamente, repetible cada 20–30 min según respuesta, **máximo 2 mg en la primera hora**, con supervisión toxicológica. Evitar con QRS ancho, sospecha de tricíclicos/bloqueo de sodio, bradicardia o trastorno de conducción.
- **Alcohol etílico:** soporte, glucosa si hipoglucemia; tiamina en riesgo de déficit antes o junto a carbohidratos cuando sea factible. No existe antídoto para la intoxicación etílica aguda.

### Analgésicos y fármacos frecuentes
- **Paracetamol/acetaminofén:** usar nomograma solo para ingesta aguda única con tiempo fiable y concentración obtenida a partir de 4 h. Iniciar **N-acetilcisteína (NAC)** si nivel en/sobre línea de tratamiento, en lesión hepática compatible o si retrasar el nivel haría iniciar >8 h tras una ingesta claramente tóxica. Usar un régimen IV/VO validado que aporte **al menos 300 mg/kg en las primeras 20–24 h**; si se usa el régimen IV clásico: 150 mg/kg en ≥1 h, 50 mg/kg en 4 h, 100 mg/kg en 16 h. No suspender por reloj: exigir criterios de parada (paracetamol bajo/indetectable, AST/ALT e INR en evolución favorable y paciente clínicamente estable).
- **Salicilatos:** no existe antídoto molecular. Alcalinizar suero/orina con bicarbonato y potasio según gasometría/electrolitos; evitar intubación si puede mantenerse ventilación espontánea. Hemodiálisis en toxicidad grave, especialmente alteración mental, hipoxemia, fracaso del tratamiento, acidosis grave o niveles altos según EXTRIP.
- **Tricíclicos y otros bloqueadores de canal de sodio (incluye difenhidramina grave, flecainida, propafenona y cocaína):** **bicarbonato sódico 1–2 mEq/kg IV** ante QRS ancho, hipotensión o arritmia ventricular, repetir guiado por respuesta y pH; evitar sobrealcalinización/hipernatremia. Lidocaína puede ser rescate especializado en arritmia ventricular persistente.
- **Bupropión:** sin antídoto; benzodiacepinas para convulsiones, soporte hemodinámico, considerar bicarbonato solo si existe bloqueo de sodio documentado (puede responder poco), ILE/ECMO en colapso refractario con toxicología.
- **Litio:** sin antídoto. Suspender, corregir volumen/electrolitos cuidadosamente y usar hemodiálisis según EXTRIP (síntomas neurológicos graves/arritmia, deterioro renal con nivel elevado o cinética de eliminación desfavorable).
- **Valproato:** soporte; considerar **L-carnitina 100 mg/kg IV (máx. 6 g) de carga, luego 15 mg/kg IV cada 4 h (máximo total 12 g/24 h)** en hiperamonemia, hepatotoxicidad, encefalopatía o intoxicación grave; considerar ECTR en toxicidad extrema según toxicología/EXTRIP.
- **Isoniazida:** convulsiones/acidosis refractarias requieren **piridoxina (vitamina B6) gramo por gramo de isoniazida ingerida**; si cantidad desconocida, referencia adulta **5 g IV en unos 10 minutos o hasta controlar convulsiones**, según referencia CAPQ, junto con benzodiacepinas y soporte; si cesan antes, acordar cómo completar la dosis con toxicología.
- **Sulfonilureas:** dextrosa para hipoglucemia + **octreótido 50 microgramos SC/IV cada 6 h**, ajustando dosis/intervalo por respuesta según toxicología en adulto para prevenir recurrencia; monitorización prolongada. En pediatría usar protocolo por peso/centro toxicológico.
- **Insulina:** no antídoto específico; dextrosa titulada (bolos + perfusión) y potasio/magnesio/fosfato seriados; evitar sobrecorrección de potasio.
- **Metformina:** no antídoto; soporte y hemodiálisis precoz en acidosis láctica grave (EXTRIP: umbral fuerte con lactato >20 mmol/L, pH ≤7.0 o fracaso de soporte; umbral menor si shock/IRA/fallo hepático).
- **Metotrexato a dosis altas con eliminación retardada/IRA:** **glucarpidasa 50 unidades/kg IV una vez durante 5 minutos** (ficha EMA) cuando cumpla criterios específicos; leucovorina debe continuar, pero no administrarla en las 2 h anteriores ni posteriores a glucarpidasa (ficha EMA). No usar glucarpidasa de forma empírica para toda sobredosis oral.
- **Colchicina:** no antídoto universalmente disponible; descontaminación precoz seleccionada, soporte intensivo y toxicología. Anticuerpos Fab específicos no son tratamiento rutinariamente disponible.
- **Digoxina y glucósidos cardiacos:** **fragmentos Fab antidigoxina** para arritmia amenazante, hiperpotasemia significativa o toxicidad grave. Dosificar por cantidad ingerida o concentración estable cuando sea posible; si no puede calcularse y existe inestabilidad vital, usar el esquema empírico del producto/centro toxicológico. No interpretar digoxinemia tras Fab con inmunoensayos convencionales como concentración libre.

### Cardiovasculares
- **Beta-bloqueadores:** soporte + vasopresores según fenotipo. **Insulina euglucémica a dosis altas**: referencia inicial 1 U/kg IV y luego 1 U/kg/h, con dextrosa para euglucemia y monitorización intensiva de glucosa/potasio; titular bajo protocolo toxicológico. **Glucagón 5–10 mg IV** puede probarse, si responde, seleccionar perfusión con toxicología: AHA2025 recoge 1–15 mg/h, ajustada al efecto; no importar la dosis de hipoglucemia; náuseas/vómitos frecuentes.
- **Calcioantagonistas:** calcio IV + **insulina euglucémica a dosis altas** precoz (1 U/kg IV, luego 1 U/kg/h y titulación), dextrosa, vasopresores; considerar ILE/ECMO en shock refractario. Calcio: usar concentración/producto local y monitorizar calcio ionizado; no intercambiar cloruro y gluconato mg por mg.
- **Clonidina/agonistas alfa-2:** soporte; naloxona puede ensayarse en casos seleccionados, especialmente pediatría, pero respuesta inconsistente.
- **Nitratos/nitritos:** si metahemoglobinemia significativa, tratar como metahemoglobinemia.
- **Antiarrítmicos clase I:** tratar bloqueo de sodio con bicarbonato; considerar lidocaína/ILE/ECMO según agente y toxicología.

### Tóxicos celulares, gases y metahemoglobina
- **Cianuro:** tratar inmediatamente si sospecha clínica grave. **Hidroxocobalamina 5 g IV** en adulto, habitualmente durante ~15 min; puede repetirse una segunda dosis de 5 g según gravedad/respuesta. Alternativa/adyuvante según toxicología: nitrito sódico + tiosulfato sódico bajo protocolo específico (Nithiodote); el nitrito puede agravar hipoxia por metahemoglobinemia, especialmente en humo/CO, anemia o reserva cardiopulmonar reducida. No administrar simultáneamente nitrito/tiosulfato por la misma vía que Cyanokit (incompatibilidad EMA). No esperar confirmación analítica.
- **Monóxido de carbono:** **100% O2**; no existe antídoto farmacológico específico. Consultar medicina hiperbárica para pérdida de conciencia, síntomas neurológicos/cardiacos, acidosis grave, embarazo con exposición significativa u otros criterios locales.
- **Metahemoglobinemia:** **azul de metileno 1–2 mg/kg IV durante 5 min**; verificar la concentración exacta: la ficha EMA de Proveblue es **5 mg/mL**, no asumir solución 1%. Una repetición se valora a 1 h según respuesta/ficha. Proveblue está **contraindicado en déficit G6PD**; evitar interacciones serotoninérgicas. La ficha limita el curso a 7 mg/kg, a 4 mg/kg en anilina/dapsona, a 2 mg/kg acumulados con eGFR 30–59 y a una dosis única 1 mg/kg con eGFR 15–29; no usar el máximo general en insuficiencia renal. Confirmar otras contraindicaciones del producto con toxicología.
- **Sulfuro de hidrógeno:** rescate seguro, O2/ventilación y soporte; no existe antídoto universal de eficacia establecida. Nitritos se han usado, pero no deben retrasar soporte y requieren toxicología.
- **Humos de incendio:** evaluar simultáneamente CO y cianuro; no asumir que una COHb baja excluye cianuro.

### Alcoholes tóxicos y solventes
- **Metanol / etilenglicol:** **fomepizol 15 mg/kg IV de carga, luego 10 mg/kg cada 12 h por 4 dosis y después 15 mg/kg cada 12 h** hasta criterios de suspensión: este esquema corresponde a la ficha estadounidense contrastada. Comprobar el producto: el SmPC de Fomepizole Waymade para etilenglicol contiene mantenimiento y ajuste en hemodiálisis diferentes; no mezclar pautas ni extrapolar a CRRT. Ajustar durante hemodiálisis según la ficha seleccionada y toxicología. Añadir folato/ácido folínico en metanol y tiamina/piridoxina en etilenglicol según protocolo. Hemodiálisis según EXTRIP por acidosis/daño de órgano/nivel/cinética.
- **Isopropanol:** no usar fomepizol de rutina; soporte. Hemodiálisis solo en casos excepcionales graves.
- **Dietilenglicol/propilenglicol:** soporte, toxicología; bloqueo de alcohol-deshidrogenasa y ECTR pueden ser considerados según agente y gravedad, sin extrapolar automáticamente el protocolo de metanol.

### Pesticidas, plantas y toxinas
- **Organofosforados/carbamatos:** descontaminación del personal/paciente, vía aérea y **atropina IV titulada rápidamente hasta secar secreciones bronquiales y mejorar ventilación/perfusión**; en adulto puede iniciarse 1–2 mg IV y duplicar cada 3–5 min si persiste síndrome muscarínico grave. **Pralidoxima** en organofosforados: referencia adulta 1–2 g IV en 15–30 min, seguida de perfusión/dosis repetidas según protocolo. Las necesidades de atropina pueden ser muy altas.
- **Rodenticidas anticoagulantes (warfarina/superwarfarinas):** vitamina K1 según INR y sangrado; las superwarfarinas pueden requerir cursos prolongados de vitamina K guiados por INR y toxicología. Hemorragia vital: seguir ruta VKA con 4F-PCC + vitamina K IV.
- **Paraquat/diquat:** no antídoto específico. Evitar hiperoxia innecesaria salvo hipoxemia; descontaminación precoz seleccionada, soporte y toxicología.
- **Estricnina:** sin antídoto; control agresivo de estímulos, benzodiacepinas y ventilación/parálisis si precisa.
- **Amanita phalloides:** soporte, NAC y silibinina IV cuando esté disponible según toxicología/hepatología; derivación precoz a centro de trasplante si insuficiencia hepática. La ficha de **Legalon SIL** (junio 2025) distingue 528,5 mg de sal de **350 mg de silibinina por vial**; no sustituir por silimarina oral. Seleccionar pauta y preparación de la ficha del producto con toxicología, sin equiparar masa de sal y principio activo.
- **Toxinas marinas (tetrodotoxina, saxitoxina, ciguatera):** predominantemente soporte; no existe antídoto específico validado para tetrodotoxina/saxitoxina. Ciguatera: tratamiento sintomático; manitol no es antídoto demostrado.
- **Mordeduras de serpiente / arañas / escorpiones:** usar **antiveneno específico del producto y especie/síndrome**, nunca una dosis genérica. Manejar coagulopatía, neurotoxicidad y shock según protocolo regional. **Viperfav** (F(ab')2 equinos) y **ViperaTAb** (Fab ovinos) tienen dosis iniciales diferentes, no intercambiables por vial o volumen. **Anascorp** es específico de Centruroides y **Antivenin Latrodectus mactans** del cuadro correspondiente; no extrapolar a cualquier escorpión/araña. Seleccionar por especie/síndrome, región y producto con toxicología, y preparar tratamiento de anafilaxia/enfermedad sérica.
- **Botulismo:** tratar precozmente la sospecha sintomática tras consulta de salud pública/toxicología. Para **BAT heptavalente equina A–G**, la ficha indica **un vial sólo como dosis de adulto desde 17 años**; niños y lactantes tienen pautas específicas del producto, nunca un vial adulto universal. **BabyBIG**, inmunoglobulina humana, corresponde al botulismo infantil A/B en menores de un año y no es intercambiable con BAT. No esperar confirmación analítica para consultar/tratar un caso compatible; vigilar hipersensibilidad durante administración.

### Metales, cáusticos y otros
- **Hierro:** **deferoxamina 15 mg/kg/h IV** en toxicidad sistémica grave, shock, acidosis o niveles indicativos; titular/duración con toxicología y vigilar hipotensión/ARDS. La referencia adulta CAPQ recoge límite de ficha 6 g/24 h; superarlo o prolongar más de 24 h requiere recomendación expresa del centro toxicológico.
- **Plomo:** quelación según nivel/síntomas. En intoxicación grave/encefalopatía seleccionar **edetato cálcico disódico (CaNa2EDTA)** solo o combinado según OMS y toxicología; dimercaprol + CaNa2EDTA es una opción, no una obligación universal. No confundir con edetato disódico sin calcio. La ficha de CaNa2EDTA contraindica anuria, enfermedad renal activa o hepatitis y advierte sobre infusión rápida en encefalopatía. **Succímero/CHEMET** oral: la ficha estadounidense es pediátrica, no indicada para encefalopatía; no atribuir autorización adulta portuguesa y vigilar hemograma/transaminasas. Dosis, vía y combinación requieren selección especializada.
- **Arsénico/mercurio:** identificar especie química, agudeza y función renal antes de seleccionar quelante con toxicología. **Dimercaprol/BAL in Oil** es IM profundo, contiene aceite de cacahuete y su ficha incluye arsénico y sales de mercurio agudas; no extrapolar a todas las formas orgánicas/crónicas. No utilizar BAL en hierro, cadmio o selenio. **DMPS/Dimaval** oral tiene indicaciones propias de mercurio y plomo crónico, diferentes de su forma inyectable y de succímero; no tratarlos como equivalentes.
- **Talio:** **azul de Prusia insoluble farmacéutico** (p. ej. Radiogardase oral) favorece eliminación gastrointestinal; no sustituir por pigmento industrial ni azul de metileno. Vigilar tránsito/estreñimiento y electrolitos; dosis/duración según producto y toxicología. Considerar ECTR en casos graves según EXTRIP.
- **Ácido fluorhídrico/fluoruros:** irrigación inmediata; **gel de gluconato cálcico 2.5%** para exposición cutánea y calcio IV/local según toxicidad, ECG y electrolitos. Las técnicas de infiltración/intraarterial requieren protocolo experto.
- **Cáusticos:** no neutralizar, no inducir vómito y no dar carbón por rutina. Evaluar vía aérea y endoscopia/imagen según lesión; manejo quirúrgico/GI temprano.
- **Hidrocarburos:** soporte respiratorio; evitar emesis/carbón rutinario por aspiración. Tratar arritmias y neumonitis según fisiología.

## urologic-emergencies

- Threats: infected obstruction, torsion, retention with complications, Fournier gangrene, priapism and major hematuria with clot retention.
- Obtain urinalysis/culture, renal function, pregnancy test and targeted ultrasound/CT. Analgesia must not delay torsion or source-control pathways.
- Infected obstruction requires antibiotics, resuscitation and urgent drainage consultation; discharge only uncomplicated stable disease with follow-up and return precautions.

## endocrine-metabolic

- Adult recognition/routing framework: consider DKA/HHS, adrenal crisis, thyroid storm, myxedema coma, severe hypoglycemia and pituitary emergencies. Assess airway, ventilation, perfusion, mental state, temperature and bedside glucose immediately. Look for infection, interrupted replacement therapy, medication effects, pregnancy and other precipitants; children require pediatric protocols.
- Obtain targeted ketones, blood gas, electrolytes, renal function and osmolality, with cortisol/ACTH or thyroid tests when indicated. Draw samples promptly if feasible, but do not delay treatment of suspected adrenal crisis or severe hypoglycemia for laboratory confirmation. Modest glucose does not exclude SGLT2-associated/euglycemic DKA; use ketones and acid-base findings.
- Route to `diabetic-ketoacidosis-hhs`, `adrenal-crisis`, `thyroid-storm` or `myxedema-coma` for their actual criteria, treatment sequence and monitoring. Do not interchange DKA, isolated HHS and routine ICU hyperglycemia regimens. Verify potassium, weight, renal function and the exact insulin/electrolyte preparation before calculation; the umbrella does not enable blocked medication contracts. In suspected myxedema coma, preserve glucocorticoid cover before thyroid replacement as specified in its module.
- Hypoglycemia: give oral fast-acting carbohydrate only when the patient is alert enough to swallow safely; impaired consciousness, seizures or unsafe swallowing require the monitored parenteral rescue pathway. Use verified IV glucose, or an appropriate glucagon alternative when IV access is unavailable, with product-specific instructions. Recheck glucose after 10--15 minutes and repeat/escalate until recovered; glucagon may fail with depleted glycogen, liver disease or sulfonylurea-related events. Review recurrence risk, provide ongoing carbohydrate when safe, and arrange supervised diabetes-treatment adjustment rather than automatically omitting all basal insulin in type 1 diabetes. Severe/recurrent or long-acting drug-related events need observation/admission and specialist/toxicology advice.
- Suspect pituitary apoplexy with sudden severe headache plus visual loss, field defect, ophthalmoplegia or altered consciousness; exclude competing neurologic emergencies in parallel. Assess visual function, stabilize, obtain urgent pituitary MRI when feasible (CT if MRI cannot be performed), and discuss immediately with endocrinology/neurosurgery. Hemodynamic instability, altered consciousness or major visual compromise warrants urgent glucocorticoid cover; sampling/imaging must not delay it. Surgery is a specialist decision, not automatic for every suspected apoplexy.
- Reassess circulation, glucose, neurological status, urine output and electrolyte trends after treatment. Admit/escalate shock, respiratory failure, impaired consciousness, major electrolyte disturbance or suspected endocrine crisis; apparent correction of one value is not discharge clearance. Document the precipitant, ongoing replacement/insulin plan, handover and follow-up.

## adrenal-crisis

- Suspect adrenal crisis with unexplained shock, vomiting, weakness, hypoglycemia or hyponatremia, especially after steroid interruption or with known adrenal/pituitary disease. Hyperkalemia may be absent in secondary adrenal insufficiency. Draw cortisol/ACTH if immediately feasible; do not delay treatment for sampling or results.
- Adult treatment: hydrocortisone 100 mg IV immediately (IM if IV access is delayed), then 200 mg over 24 hours or 50 mg IV/IM every 6 hours. If pharmacy confirms the product/diluent and stability, 200 mg to a final 100 mL = 2 mg/mL, delivering 8.33 mg/h at 4.17 mL/h; 200 mg/200 mL = 1 mg/mL at 8.33 mL/h. Confirm bag replacement interval; never assume 24-hour stability from this arithmetic.
- Restore circulation with isotonic saline; an adult without fluid-overload risk commonly receives 1 L in the first hour, then reassessment. Use smaller reassessed aliquots in cardiac/renal disease. Treat hypoglycemia promptly, monitor sodium correction, glucose, potassium, urine output and perfusion, and treat the precipitant.
- The intermittent hydrocortisone regimen is an alternative when a pump is unavailable. If hydrocortisone itself is unavailable, urgently obtain an endocrinology/pharmacy-verified parenteral glucocorticoid alternative; do not substitute milligram-for-milligram or assume equal mineralocorticoid effect. Admit, taper after recovery with endocrinology and provide sick-day education.

## thyroid-storm

- Suspect clinical thyrotoxic decompensation with fever, marked tachycardia/arrhythmia, CNS disturbance, heart failure or GI/hepatic dysfunction. Scores support diagnosis but should not delay ICU/endocrine treatment. Obtain thyroid tests, glucose, electrolytes, liver tests, ECG, cultures and cardiac assessment while treating the precipitant.
- Control hormone synthesis when due to hyperfunction: ATA adult reference regimens are propylthiouracil 500--1,000 mg enteral loading then 250 mg every 4 hours, OR methimazole/thiamazole 60--80 mg/day in divided enteral doses. Select around hepatic injury, pregnancy, contraindications and actual availability; do not combine automatically. Thyroiditis/exogenous hormone requires a different strategy.
- Both ATA 2016 and the 2026 joint thyroid-storm consensus specify iodine at least 1 hour after the thionamide. Verify iodine concentration and formulation before converting to drops; products are not interchangeable. Other guideline sequences require explicit specialist reconciliation.
- Give stress-dose glucocorticoid using one verified storm protocol. The 2026 ETA/BTA/Society for Endocrinology/Welsh Endocrine and Diabetes Society consensus proposes hydrocortisone 100 mg IV once, then 50 mg IV every 6 hours (or 200 mg over 24 hours by syringe driver in HDU/ICU). ATA 2016 lists a different regimen (300 mg IV loading, then 100 mg every 8 hours); do not combine or silently interchange regimens—follow the current specialist/local protocol. Monitor potassium after glucocorticoids, especially when thyrotoxic periodic paralysis is plausible. Do not confuse storm treatment with adrenal-crisis dosing. Provide external cooling and suitable antipyresis; avoid aspirin/salicylates. Manage fluids and oxygen according to cardiac status.
- Assess shock and ventricular function BEFORE beta-blockade. Decompensated low-output failure can collapse after propranolol or even esmolol. In a well-perfused patient, select/titrate a verified beta-blocker regimen with continuous monitoring. If esmolol is appropriate, use the existing preparation/calculation in `acute-aortic-syndrome`, but do not copy its aortic BP/HR targets. Avoid automatic loading in tenuous circulation; stop for worsening perfusion, bradycardia, block or bronchospasm. Diltiazem is not automatically safe in systolic failure either.
- If significant hepatic dysfunction is present (e.g. transaminases around >3 times the upper limit of normal or rising bilirubin), the 2026 consensus favours considering methimazole/carbimazole over PTU because of PTU hepatotoxicity risk, except where patient-specific factors such as first-trimester pregnancy alter selection. Observational data have not shown a clear mortality advantage for PTU. Once clinically improving, reduce thionamide dosing as directed by endocrinology. If not clinically stabilising within 24–48 hours, reassess precipitant, absorption, cardiac dysfunction and need for rescue. Cholestyramine, lithium, plasma exchange or thyroidectomy are specialist adjunct/rescue options, not automatic steps. If thionamides are unavailable/contraindicated or treatment fails, obtain urgent specialist rescue planning; do not replace them with an unrelated antihypertensive. Verify Portuguese products via the localization workflow.

## myxedema-coma

- Recognize severe decompensated hypothyroidism with altered consciousness, hypothermia, hypoventilation, bradycardia, hypotension and/or hyponatremia; coma is not required. TSH may not be elevated with central disease. Obtain TSH/free T4, cortisol, gas, glucose/electrolytes and precipitant assessment without delaying treatment.
- Give empiric stress-dose hydrocortisone BEFORE thyroid hormone while adrenal insufficiency is being excluded; the adrenal-crisis module supplies 100 mg IV followed by 200 mg/24 h or 50 mg every 6 hours. Reconcile ongoing doses with the endocrine team.
- Adult ATA reference: levothyroxine 200--400 micrograms IV ONCE as a loading dose, reduced for older/smaller patients, coronary disease or arrhythmia. Subsequent daily IV replacement is approximately 75% of an oral 1.6 micrograms/kg/day reference, individualized with specialist monitoring. Never repeat the loading dose as daily maintenance or confuse micrograms with milligrams.
- If IV levothyroxine is unavailable, arrange urgent endocrine/pharmacy-directed enteral treatment; absorption with ileus is unreliable and oral and IV doses are not equivalent. Liothyronine is a selected specialist adjunct, not a routine substitute; cardiac risk and local availability matter.
- Admit to ICU, support ventilation early if needed, use cautious fluids and passive warming, correct hypoglycemia and sodium safely, and treat infection/other triggers. Avoid aggressive peripheral warming and unnecessary sedatives. Follow consciousness, ventilation, temperature, ECG/perfusion and free hormone trends.
- The 2026 ETA/BTA/Society for Endocrinology/Welsh Endocrine and Diabetes Society joint consensus supports IV levothyroxine loading at 200–400 micrograms, reducing the dose in older/smaller patients and those with ischaemic heart disease or significant arrhythmia, plus empiric IV hydrocortisone 100 mg then 50 mg every 6 hours (or 200 mg/24 hours by syringe driver). This agrees with the module's cautious loading and hydrocortisone schedule. The exact enteral fallback regimen when IV levothyroxine is unavailable still requires source-level and pharmacy/local-protocol confirmation; do not invent an oral/NG loading dose or claim it is dose-equivalent.

## diabetic-ketoacidosis-hhs

- Diagnose adult DKA only when all three domains are present: diabetes/history or glucose at least 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L), ketosis with beta-hydroxybutyrate at least 3.0 mmol/L or urine ketones at least 2+, and metabolic acidosis with pH below 7.3 or bicarbonate below 18 mmol/L. Actively consider euglycemic DKA with SGLT2 inhibitors, pregnancy, fasting or partial insulin treatment; do not exclude DKA from glucose alone.
- Diagnose HHS only when all four domains are present: glucose at least 600 mg/dL (33.3 mmol/L), effective osmolality above 300 mOsm/kg or total calculated osmolality above 320 mOsm/kg, beta-hydroxybutyrate below 3.0 mmol/L or urine ketones below 2+, and pH at least 7.3 with bicarbonate at least 15 mmol/L. Classify significant ketonaemia/acidosis with hyperosmolality as mixed DKA/HHS.
- Obtain glucose, beta-hydroxybutyrate, venous blood gas, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, urea/creatinine, magnesium, phosphate, measured osmolality when available, ECG, fluid balance and precipitant evaluation. Calculate anion gap, albumin-corrected gap when albumin is known, effective/total osmolality and corrected sodium. Prefer beta-hydroxybutyrate to urine ketones and use `scripts/acid_base_hyperglycemia.py` for deterministic arithmetic.
- Restore perfusion first. In adults without cardiac or renal compromise, give isotonic saline or a balanced crystalloid at 500--1000 mL/h for the first 2--4 hours, then individualize by hemodynamics, sodium, osmolality and fluid balance. In older adults, pregnancy, heart failure or kidney failure, use smaller aliquots such as 250 mL with frequent reassessment; do not apply a fixed large-volume protocol.
- Check potassium before insulin. If potassium is below 3.5 mmol/L, replace potassium at 10 mmol/h and delay insulin until potassium is above 3.5 mmol/L. One conditional reference preparation is potassium chloride 20 mmol in a compatible final volume of 100 mL = 0.2 mmol/mL, delivered at 50 mL/h = 10 mmol/h; use only when the local protocol permits that concentration for the verified access, with a controlled pump and ECG/electrolyte monitoring. Once potassium falls below 5.0 mmol/L, usually add 20--30 mmol potassium per litre of IV fluid to target 4--5 mmol/L; withhold initial potassium when elevated and monitor closely. Never IV-push potassium.
- For DKA or mixed DKA/HHS, use soluble/regular insulin IV at 0.1 units/kg/h after the potassium safety gate. A practical pump preparation is 100 units in a final volume of 100 mL compatible 0.9% saline = 1 unit/mL: the numeric units/h equals mL/h. Prime tubing according to local protocol. Do not routinely give an insulin bolus when the infusion can start promptly.
- When glucose falls below 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) in DKA, add 5--10% dextrose and reduce insulin to 0.05 units/kg/h, targeting glucose near 200 mg/dL while continuing insulin until ketoacidosis resolves. In euglycemic DKA, begin dextrose with the insulin pathway rather than withholding insulin because glucose is near normal.
- In HHS without significant ketosis/acidosis, prioritize controlled fluid/osmolality correction and use insulin IV at 0.05 units/kg/h; a fluid-only glucose fall may occur before insulin. Do not exceed a glucose decline of 90--120 mg/dL/h, a sodium fall of 10 mmol/L/24 h or an osmolality fall of 3--8 mOsm/kg/h. An initial sodium rise as glucose falls is expected and alone is not an indication for hypotonic saline.
- Check capillary glucose every 1--2 hours. Repeat electrolytes, creatinine, phosphate, beta-hydroxybutyrate and venous pH every 4 hours; in HHS also repeat osmolality every 4 hours. Recheck potassium 2 hours after insulin starts and at least every 4 hours, more often when unstable or actively replacing.
- Assess bleeding, renal function, weight and venous/arterial thrombosis. Unless thrombosis is suspected or anticoagulation is contraindicated, use prophylactic-dose low-molecular-weight heparin according to the locally available product and renal/weight protocol; do not escalate automatically to therapeutic anticoagulation merely because HHS is prothrombotic.
- Do not give bicarbonate routinely. Consider it only for severe DKA acidosis below pH 7.0 under a current protocol; if used, the 2024 consensus regimen is sodium bicarbonate 100 mmol in 400 mL sterile water every 2 hours until pH exceeds 7.0, with potassium and sodium monitoring. Do not replace phosphate routinely unless phosphate below 1.0 mmol/L is accompanied by respiratory/cardiac muscle weakness or another specific indication.
- Define DKA resolution by beta-hydroxybutyrate below 0.6 mmol/L plus venous pH at least 7.3 or bicarbonate at least 18 mmol/L; ideally glucose is below 200 mg/dL. Do not use anion gap or urine ketones alone because recovery hyperchloremic acidosis and ketone conversion can mislead. Consider HHS resolved when osmolality is below 300 mOsm/kg, cognition and hyperglycemia improve, urine output exceeds 0.5 mL/kg/h and glucose is below 250 mg/dL.
- Continue or initiate an appropriate basal insulin plan. For transition after DKA/HHS, administer the subcutaneous basal insulin 2--4 hours before stopping IV insulin (ADA 2026 section 16); verify the product, documented administration time and ongoing basal coverage, including any basal already given. This basal-specific rule replaces the older general 1--2 hour SC-overlap shorthand from the 2024 consensus in this pathway. Stop IV insulin only after crisis resolution and with a verified basal/nutritional/correction plan; glucose normalization alone is insufficient. Do not infer a universal transition percentage, extra basal dose or NPH-specific interval. Treat the precipitant, stop SGLT2 inhibitors during the event, and admit severe DKA, HHS, mixed crises, altered consciousness, shock or major comorbidity to a high-acuity setting.

## acid-base-emergencies

- Confirm sample type (arterial, venous or capillary), oxygen delivery/FiO2, collection time and clinical context. Venous pH/bicarbonate are often sufficient for DKA trending, but venous PO2 must not be used to assess arterial oxygenation; use pulse oximetry and an arterial sample when oxygenation or an arterial-venous discrepancy matters.
- Separate the current result, reference interval and comparative column before interpretation; do not infer a prior result's time from a pasted layout. Use actual plasma bicarbonate (cHCO3(P)), not standard bicarbonate (cHCO3(P,st)), for simultaneous gap/compensation calculations. Read pH, PCO2 and bicarbonate together. Then obtain sodium, chloride, albumin, lactate, beta-hydroxybutyrate, glucose, urea/creatinine and relevant toxins. A normal pH does not exclude a mixed disorder.
- **Units and gap convention:** gas pressures must be explicitly mmHg or kPa; 1 kPa is approximately 7.50062 mmHg. Electrolytes/bicarbonate are mmol/L. Albumin g/L must be divided by 10 before using a g/dL formula; hemoglobin reported in g/dL does not establish the laboratory's albumin unit. Calculate anion gap as `Na - (Cl + HCO3)` and, when albumin is known in g/dL, corrected gap as `AG + 2.5 x (4 - albumin)`. The alternative potassium-inclusive gap is `Na + K - Cl - HCO3` and requires its own verified reference interval. Never apply an unidentified analyzer gap interval to a calculation that excludes potassium. For established high-gap metabolic acidosis calculate the delta ratio `(corrected AG - matching normal AG) / (24 - HCO3)`; 12 mmol/L without potassium is a generic reference, not a verified local baseline. State whether albumin correction was possible. Omit the ratio when bicarbonate is >=24 or the corrected gap is not elevated; near-zero denominators and variable baseline gap make isolated cutoffs unreliable.
- After confirming a metabolic-acidosis component, calculate Winter compensation: expected PCO2 `1.5 x HCO3 + 8 +/- 2 mmHg`. A measured PCO2 above this range indicates an added respiratory acidosis; below it indicates an added respiratory alkalosis. Low bicarbonate alone may instead reflect compensation for primary respiratory alkalosis. The calculator exposes the Winter label as conditional unless `metabolic_acidosis_confirmed` is explicitly true. For metabolic alkalosis use expected PCO2 approximately `40 + 0.7 x (HCO3 - 24) +/- 5`.
- For respiratory disorders, compare acute and chronic expected bicarbonate responses instead of declaring chronicity from one gas: respiratory acidosis raises bicarbonate by about 1 mmol/L per 10 mmHg acute and 3.5--4 chronic; respiratory alkalosis lowers it by about 2 acute and 4--5 chronic.
- A near-normal pH may conceal a double or triple disorder. Combine the primary clinical process, gap/albumin, delta relationship and respiratory compensation; an abnormal delta ratio is a clue, not an automatic extra diagnosis. Obtain missing albumin, sample type, medication/exposure history and trends rather than inventing them.
- Use `scripts/acid_base_hyperglycemia.py` for reproducible calculations with explicit unit fields; duplicate-unit fields, nonfinite values and mathematically impossible inputs are rejected. Conversion and arithmetic do not establish analyzer accuracy or diagnose the cause. Treat the patient and cause: restore perfusion/oxygenation, address sepsis, DKA, renal failure, diarrhea, vomiting, salicylate/toxic alcohol exposure or ventilatory failure. Do not administer bicarbonate solely because bicarbonate is low. Route treatment to the verified syndrome pathway. BICARICU-2 (2025) found no reduction in 90-day mortality with bicarbonate in severe metabolic acidemia plus moderate/severe AKI; this does not establish a universal bicarbonate indication or contraindication. Shock, DKA, toxicology and renal-replacement decisions retain their own criteria.
- In severe metabolic acidosis, avoid unnecessary intubation. If intubation is unavoidable, minimize apnea and match or initially approximate the pre-intubation minute ventilation; an abrupt PCO2 rise can cause profound acidemia and cardiovascular collapse. Obtain an early post-intubation gas and reassess ventilation.

## aki-nephrology

- Determine baseline, perfusion, obstruction, nephrotoxins, urinalysis findings and complications. Use bladder/renal ultrasound when obstruction is plausible.
- Treat shock and cause while avoiding both under-resuscitation and congestion; adjust renally cleared drugs.
- Escalate refractory hyperkalemia, severe acidosis, pulmonary edema, uremic complication or toxin indication for nephrology/dialysis; do not use creatinine alone to decide.
- **Version control14/11/2026:** the official KDIGO AKI/AKD page still describes2026 as a public-review draft under preparation for publication. Retain the published KDIGO2012 baseline; do not label draft biomarker definitions, treatment thresholds or recommendations as final2026 guidance.
- **Recognition:** AKI includes any creatinine rise>=0.3mg/dL (26.5micromol/L) within48h, >=1.5times baseline within7days, or urine output<0.5mL/kg/h for6h. Check trends and the actual baseline; oliguria can precede creatinine elevation.
- **Urgent management:** investigate obstruction, sepsis, hypoperfusion and nephrotoxins; review drug doses against changing renal function. Use diuretics for volume overload when indicated, not to reverse AKI itself; do not use low-dose dopamine for renal protection. Life-threatening electrolyte, acid-base or fluid complications require urgent renal replacement assessment without a creatinine-only threshold. Arrange post-AKI follow-up, including reassessment around3months for recovery/CKD.

## rhabdomyolysis

- Identify trauma/compression, exertion/heat, seizure, drugs/toxins and ischemia; check CK trend, potassium, calcium, phosphate, renal function and ECG.
- Give goal-directed isotonic crystalloid while tracking urine output, perfusion and congestion; a commonly used adult urine-output target is about 1--3 mL/kg/h (up to roughly 300 mL/h), but stop escalating fluid if oliguria persists with overload risk. Treat hyperkalemia urgently and trend CK until a clear peak/downtrend.
- Do not use bicarbonate, mannitol or loop diuretics routinely to prevent rhabdomyolysis-associated AKI; reserve them for another specific indication or specialist protocol. Do not chase CK with unlimited fluid.
- Evaluate compartment syndrome and dialysis indications; admit significant electrolyte disturbance, AKI, systemic illness or ongoing muscle injury.

## acute-liver-failure

- Recognize acute liver injury with coagulopathy and encephalopathy; assess glucose frequently and seek toxins, viral, ischemic, autoimmune and pregnancy causes.
- Start N-acetylcysteine immediately for suspected acetaminophen toxicity without waiting for a level. For adult non-acetaminophen ALF, ACG 2023 supports early IV NAC with liver/transplant specialist review; the early-encephalopathy trial signal does not establish benefit in every cause/stage or justify pediatric extrapolation. NAC does not replace cause-specific therapy or transplant assessment. Obtain an accurate timeline, acetaminophen concentration, toxicology and serial hepatic, renal, glucose, lactate, ammonia and coagulation data.
- Manage cerebral/airway risk and avoid unnecessary correction of INR without bleeding or an invasive-procedure need because INR does not measure the full hemostatic balance and correction can obscure prognosis.
- Contact a transplant-capable liver center at first recognition rather than after deterioration; worsening encephalopathy, lactate, acidosis, hypoglycemia or multiorgan failure requires critical care and urgent transplant assessment.

No receiving-center name, phone number, guaranteed transfer time or new specialist/pharmacy signature is inferred. Use the accepted local contact route immediately when ALF is suspected.

## alcohol-withdrawal

- **Adult reference:** children and pregnancy need specialist-specific management. Establish reduction/last intake, usual consumption, prior seizures/delirium tremens, concurrent sedatives/opioids, pregnancy, trauma, infection, liver disease, nutrition, glucose and electrolytes. Intoxication and withdrawal risk may coexist. Exclude alternative causes of agitation, seizure or confusion; alcohol attribution must not delay resuscitation or other urgent diagnosis.
- **Use scores appropriately:** CIWA-Ar measures symptoms, not a stand-alone diagnosis. Reliable reporting and trained assessment are prerequisites. Delirium, critical illness or inability to communicate requires a monitored clinician-directed/objective approach such as RASS/MINDS rather than CIWA-Ar-driven dosing.
- **Benzodiazepines are first-line:** choose symptom-triggered treatment only when a reliable validated assessment can be performed with trained staffing, or a prescribed fixed/front-loading regimen with frequent reassessment. Individualize for age, liver disease, previous sedatives and respiratory risk; lorazepam/oxazepam may be preferable when long-acting active metabolites are problematic. Do not apply a routine unsupervised reducing schedule to severe complicated withdrawal.
- **ASAM monitored adult example:** diazepam **20 mg orally every 2 h for 3 doses**. Reassess before each dose; hold for oversedation, hypoventilation or hypotension. This is **not a compulsory 60 mg course** or universal maximum; do not stack loading regimens. Frailty, liver disease, unsafe swallowing or cardiorespiratory risk requires individualization.
- **Thiamine phases, UK acute-hospital guidance 2025/updated 2026:** lower-risk prophylaxis **50 mg orally four times daily**; high-risk prophylaxis **200--300 mg IV or IM once daily for 3--5 days**, with daily review. Suspected/established Wernicke encephalopathy requires **300--500 mg IV three times daily for 3--5 days**; if still symptomatic after the first 5 days, continue 300--500 mg IV daily for a further 3--5 days and while improvement continues, under review. Do not substitute prophylaxis for treatment of suspected Wernicke disease or wait for a complete classic triad. Follow parenteral treatment with appropriate oral supplementation.
- Give thiamine promptly but **do not delay glucose for hypoglycemia**. Check/correct Mg, K and phosphate as indicated and watch refeeding/fluid risk. Verify the actual parenteral thiamine product, dilution and administration time, with anaphylaxis treatment available; do not invent ampoule counts, IV push timing or a local formulation from the guideline dose.
- **Adjunct boundaries:** phenobarbital requires experienced staff, close monitoring and a cumulative-dose/airway plan; avoid unstructured benzodiazepine stacking. Antipsychotics, clonidine or dexmedetomidine do not replace seizure/delirium prevention. Active seizures follow the status pathway; phenytoin is not routine without another indication.
- **Monitor and escalate:** reassess symptoms, consciousness/sedation, respiratory rate, SpO2, BP/HR, temperature, fluids and electrolytes; review before/after doses and use continuous cardiorespiratory observation for severe illness or parenteral sedative escalation. Delirium tremens, recurrent seizures, refractory/high-dose requirements, respiratory depression, unstable physiology or serious comorbidity needs senior/ICU assessment and airway capability. Do not wait for a score threshold when complications occur.
- Discharge requires controlled symptoms, adequate observation after sedatives, nutrition/thiamine and a safe follow-up plan; severe/high-risk withdrawal is not routine outpatient detoxification. Offer ongoing alcohol-use-disorder treatment/support rather than treating withdrawal as the whole intervention. No new sedative infusion or automated drug contract is approved by this guide.
