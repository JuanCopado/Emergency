# Disposition and cross-cutting modules

## observation-discharge

- Define the clinical question observation must answer, required serial tests/exams, treatment endpoints and maximum safe duration.
- Discharge only when physiology and function are stable, serious diagnoses are reasonably excluded, symptoms are controlled and follow-up is feasible.
- Provide diagnosis/uncertainty, medication instructions, specific return precautions, pending-result ownership and follow-up plan; use teach-back when possible.

## hematology-oncology

- Threats include febrile neutropenia, leukostasis, tumor lysis, hyperviscosity, spinal cord compression, malignant hypercalcemia and major bleeding/thrombosis.
- Obtain treatment history, counts/smear, electrolytes, renal function, coagulation and targeted imaging. Give time-critical antimicrobials or syndrome therapy after protocol verification.
- Contact hematology/oncology early; immunosuppressed patients may deteriorate with minimal initial signs.

## ophthalmology-emergencies

- Measure visual acuity in each eye when possible, pupils, fields, movements and pressure only when globe rupture is not suspected.
- Threats include chemical injury, open globe, acute angle closure, retinal arterial occlusion/detachment, endophthalmitis and orbital cellulitis.
- Irrigate chemical exposure immediately before detailed examination. Shield suspected open globe, avoid pressure and obtain urgent ophthalmology care; verify time-sensitive drug regimens.

## ent-emergencies

- Prioritize airway, major hemorrhage, deep-neck infection, epiglottitis, mastoid/orbital spread, sudden sensorineural hearing loss and button-battery/caustic foreign bodies.
- Avoid distressing airway examination when obstruction is plausible; mobilize anesthesia/ENT and controlled-airway resources.
- Use anatomy-specific hemostasis/removal techniques with rescue plan; reassess airway, bleeding and neurovascular status before disposition.

## environmental-emergencies

- Measure core temperature when relevant and stop exposure; assess trauma, glucose, electrolytes, ECG and organ injury.
- Use syndrome-specific active cooling/rewarming while preventing shivering, burns and afterdrop; handle severe hypothermia gently and follow temperature-modified arrest guidance.
- Consider toxins, exertional heat stroke, drowning, altitude, bites/stings and electrical injury; contact toxicology/hyperbaric/specialist services as indicated.

## ecg-pocus-integration

- Start from the clinical question and pretest probability; record rhythm/rate/intervals/ischemia on ECG and focused cardiac/lung/vascular findings on POCUS.
- Reconcile discordant data rather than allowing one test to overrule the patient. Repeat studies after intervention or clinical change.
- State technical limitations and alternatives. Normal or limited studies do not exclude ACS, PE, aortic disease, early tamponade or other high-risk syndromes.

## pocus

- Define a focused question, obtain standard views, save images and document operator/technical limitations.
- Integrate findings with physiology and other testing; use serial POCUS for response and evolving disease where appropriate.
- Do not convert qualitative findings into unsupported volume status or diagnostic certainty. A single still frame cannot establish motion-dependent signs such as lung sliding; a negative or technically limited study cannot exclude a high-risk syndrome outside the validated scope of that examination.
- Record the operator, indication, views, adequacy, findings, interpretation and resulting action. Seek comprehensive imaging or expert review when the decision exceeds focused ultrasound capability.
